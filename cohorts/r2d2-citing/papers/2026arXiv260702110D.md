# Black Boxes in Black Hole Imaging

**Bibcode:** 2026arXiv260702110D
**Authors:** Juliusz Doboszewski, Jamee Elder
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260702110D/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02110

## One-line summary
A philosophy-of-science paper arguing that epistemic opacity in machine-learning-based black hole imaging methods need not undermine the reliability of scientific inference, provided the methods are embedded in a broader epistemic context that supports convergence with independent lines of evidence.

## Method
This is not a technical/quantitative imaging paper but a conceptual/philosophical analysis. The authors examine the epistemic opacity of computer simulations and ML methods used in black hole imaging (EHT observations of M87* and Sgr A*). They survey the imaging pipeline landscape — CLEAN, Regularized Maximum Likelihood (RML, via eht-imaging/SMILI), PRIMO (dictionary learning trained on GRMHD simulation snapshots), R2D2 (residual-to-residual DNN series of denoisers, noted for "deep unrolling" giving partial algorithmic transparency), and α-DPI (variational/importance-sampling Bayesian inference) — and argue that algorithmic transparency is not the right criterion for trustworthiness. Instead, they propose four conditions (C1–C4) under which opaque ML methods can be reliably used: (C1/C2) diverse, bias-tracked training data; (C3) prior validation in previously explored physical domains before novel application; (C4) convergence of ML results with independent methods/evidence. They also identify a "problematic" opacity in current GRMHD models of Sgr A* that limits how confidently ML methods trained on them can be trusted, and caution against extending such methods to genuinely novel observational domains without independent verification.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
This is not a metrics paper — no quantitative image-fidelity metrics (SNR, PSNR, SSIM, dynamic range, chi-squared/residuals, runtime, etc.) are reported or computed. Instead, "reliability" of an imaging method is characterized qualitatively:
- **Robustness/convergence across independent methods** is treated as the operative validation criterion: "image properties are considered to be reliable to the extent that they are robust across different imaging methods," and features lacking that robustness are treated as artifacts rather than trusted signal.
- Reliability is explicitly decoupled from explainability/transparency — an opaque (black-box) method can be epistemically trustworthy if it satisfies conditions C1–C4 above, rather than by inspecting its internal mechanism.
- The paper flags **bias propagation** as a fidelity concern for training-data-dependent methods: e.g., PRIMO is noted to inherit opacity and potential empirical inadequacy from the GRMHD simulations used as its training/prior set.
- R2D2's "deep unrolling" architecture is specifically noted as offering a partial mitigation to opacity (more interpretable intermediate steps) compared to fully black-box networks, though this is discussed conceptually, not measured.
- The authors argue prior validation on known/previously-observed targets (satisfying C3) functions as a stand-in for conventional fidelity benchmarking before deployment on novel targets.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
Directly relevant as a methodological/epistemological companion piece: it discusses R2D2 by name alongside CLEAN, RML, PRIMO, and α-DPI, and argues that when validating ML deconvolution/imaging pipelines, cross-method convergence and prior-domain validation should be prioritized over demanding interpretability, which is worth citing when justifying evaluation strategy choices for an ML-based imaging pipeline.
