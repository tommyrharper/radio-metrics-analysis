# A Decentralized Framework for Radio-interferometric Image Reconstruction

**Bibcode:** 2025AJ....169..289W
**Authors:** S. Wang, S. Mignot, S. Prunet, L. Di Mascolo, M. Spinelli, A. Ferrari
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025AJ....169..289W/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22128 (arXiv:2507.22128)

## One-line summary
The paper introduces a decentralized, node-parallel reconstruction framework that splits visibilities by spatial frequency (low vs. high) across compute nodes, applies it to both multi-scale CLEAN and sparsity-regularized convex (wavelet/L1, FISTA-based) imaging, and shows close-to-optimal 2× speedups on large datasets with reconstruction quality similar to the serial baselines.

## Method
Visibilities are partitioned into low-frequency (𝒱ℒ) and high-frequency (𝒱ℋ) subsets, one per node. Each node reconstructs from its local visibilities alone in the first major cycle; from the second major cycle onward, nodes exchange filtered/deconvolved residual images so each node solves a "pseudo-full-resolution" problem combining its own local data with the partner node's contribution, converging iteratively over 5 major cycles. Two deconvolution algorithms are parallelized this way: (1) multi-scale CLEAN (ms-CLEAN) with scales {0,1,2,4,6,10,30} and up to K=2000 minor-cycle iterations, and (2) a sparsity-regularized convex reconstruction (wavelet L1 minimization via FISTA, first 8 Daubechies wavelets), with an added data-fidelity term for the exchanged images in the parallel version. The framework is benchmarked on two simulated datasets (SKA-Mid "Sgr B2", SKA-Low "Sgr C") and two real datasets (ALMA "HL Tau", VLA "Cygnus A"), plus a resolution-scaling test on Cygnus A at 10k×10k pixels.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **Signal-to-noise ratio (S/N):** defined as 20·log10(‖i‖₂/‖i−î‖₂) between ground-truth image i and reconstruction î; used for the two simulated datasets (Sgr B2, Sgr C) where ground truth is known. Tracked per major cycle (Fig. 5); parallel and serial methods converge to comparable S/N.
- **Wasserstein distance (W₅):** L2 norm of per-pixel Wasserstein-1 distances computed in 5×5 sliding windows between reconstructions across iterations/methods, used as a fidelity proxy on the real datasets (HL Tau, Cygnus A) where no ground truth exists; decreases with iteration count, used to argue convergence and similarity of parallel vs. serial output images.
- **Wall-clock reconstruction time (s):** total processing time per method/dataset; e.g. serial L1 (sparse) ranged ~637–919 s, parallel p-L1 ~540–867 s across datasets (Fig. 6).
- **Speedup:** ratio of serial to parallel wall-clock time; close to the theoretical optimum of 2× (two nodes) achieved for the larger datasets (HL Tau, Cygnus A); smaller datasets (Sgr B2, Sgr C) show little to no improvement because per-cycle overhead dominates. One anomalous case for Cygnus A exceeded the 2× optimum, attributed to overhead differences.
- **Timing breakdown by pipeline stage:** degridding, gridding, deconvolution, disk I/O, inter-node idle time, and "other" (polarization conversion, memory allocation, weight computation) — decomposed per method to identify bottlenecks. Deconvolution and disk I/O overhead (not raw I/O bandwidth) dominate; idle time appears when visibility partitions are unbalanced (e.g., Sgr B2 split 75%/27%).
- **Resolution-scaling factors (Table 3, Cygnus A 1728² → 10k×10k):** deconvolution time scales ×50 (p-msc, multiscale CLEAN) / ×39 (p-L1); degridding ×16; gridding ×21–22; disk I/O ×1 (negligible/constant); inter-node image-transfer time ×52 (p-msc) / ×30 (p-L1), reaching just over 0.5 s at 10k×10k — flagged as a potential future bottleneck as node count grows (communication cost grows faster than linearly with the number of partitions).
- **Comparison to baselines:** parallel methods (p-msc, p-L1) are compared directly to their serial counterparts (msc, L1) — no comparison to R2D2/AIRI/uSARA deep-learning methods is made; baselines here are classical CLEAN and convex-optimization imaging only. p-L1 converges to images of similar quality to serial L1; p-msc was found to reconstruct slightly better (or equal-quality, faster) images than serial msc at matched wall-clock time.
- **Datasets/scale:** Sgr B2 (SKA-Mid sim., 9.36M visibilities, 512×512 @0.18″); Sgr C (SKA-Low sim., 15.76M visibilities, 512×512 @0.429″); HL Tau (ALMA Band 6, 85.5M visibilities, 1500×1500 @0.005″); Cygnus A (VLA, 82.2M visibilities, 1728×1728 @0.125″, also tested at 10k×10k).

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
Directly relevant to scalability: it demonstrates a concrete strategy for parallelizing the gridding+deconvolution stages across compute nodes by splitting visibilities in spatial-frequency space and exchanging partial reconstructions between major cycles, with explicit profiling of gridding/degridding/deconvolution/communication costs as image size grows — a useful reference architecture and cost model for scaling any future ML-based (e.g. R2D2-style) deconvolution network to large distributed datasets, even though this paper itself only parallelizes classical CLEAN and convex-optimization methods, not deep-learning ones.
