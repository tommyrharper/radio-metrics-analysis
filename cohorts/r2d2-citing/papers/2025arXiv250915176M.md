# To CLEAN or not to CLEAN: Data Processing in the ngVLA era

**Bibcode:** 2025arXiv250915176M
**Authors:** Hendrik Müller
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250915176M/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15176

## One-line summary
A perspective/review piece (IAUS 397 proceedings) arguing that CLEAN's point-source assumption and non-scalable major/minor-loop structure will struggle to meet the data volumes and dynamic-range demands of ngVLA/SKA/ALMA-WSU, and that CLEAN-hybrid methods are the best near-term fix while Bayesian and AI-based frameworks (including R2D2) represent the long-term future of interferometric imaging.

## Method
The paper is not a new algorithm but a survey/position paper covering the deconvolution stage of the standard radio-interferometric pipeline (gridding → major loop → deconvolution/minor loop → degridding, Fig. 1). It divides imaging methods into "inverse modeling" (CLEAN and its multiscale/adaptive-scale/wavelet variants — MS-CLEAN, Asp-CLEAN, DoB-CLEAN) and "forward modeling" (Regularized Maximum Likelihood, compressive sensing, Bayesian inference e.g. `resolve`/fast-resolve, and AI-driven methods e.g. R2D2, AIRI-family work from Dabbech/Wiaux, and diffusion/other deep nets). It highlights "hybrid" approaches — Autocorr-CLEAN, CG-CLEAN, PolyCLEAN, Momentum-CLEAN — that keep CLEAN's major/minor-loop workflow (and CASA compatibility) but replace its regularization/optimization with convex-optimization or conjugate-gradient schemes, giving faster convergence and better residual behavior without new infrastructure. It also flags a specific AI-assisted hybrid (Fiedler et al. 2025, in prep) that trains a network to transform single-scale Högbom CLEAN components into a multiscale representation, recovering MS-CLEAN-level accuracy with minimal overhead. Conclusion: hybrids are the near-term bridge; Bayesian/AI frameworks (R2D2, fast-resolve, etc.) are the long-term direction once large-scale validation and computational cost issues are resolved.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **Computational budgets (concrete, cited from NRAO/ALMA memos):** ALMA Wideband Sensitivity Upgrade (WSU) requires ~100 Tflop/s; ngVLA requires ~50 Petaflop/s, with **gridding identified as the dominant computational cost** (Bhatnagar et al. 2021; Kepley et al. 2024). Quantum computing is explicitly noted as not expected to solve the radio-interferometry data-size problem in the near future (Brunet et al. 2024).
- **Residual/convergence behavior (qualitative + figure-based, Cygnus A test case):** CG-CLEAN and Momentum-CLEAN are shown to drive major-loop residuals toward a noise-like distribution more efficiently than traditional CLEAN (Fig. 2, residual vs. major-loop iteration). Autocorr-CLEAN and Asp-CLEAN are shown to reduce minor-loop iteration counts and wall-clock time needed for convergence "by up to an order of magnitude" relative to Högbom CLEAN, with residual-vs-time/iteration panels reported for Cygnus A (Fig. 3; e.g. Högbom needs ~4000 iterations/227s to reach a residual level Autocorr-CLEAN reaches with far fewer/faster iterations, while Asp-CLEAN is much slower per iteration despite improved accuracy).
- **Fidelity claims (largely qualitative, no single benchmark table):** forward-modeling/Bayesian/AI methods are said to "often achieve superior fidelity for complex/extended structures," offer flexible priors and principled uncertainty quantification, but at "greater computational cost" that remains "a barrier to routine use." No explicit numeric SNR/dynamic-range/NMSE/PSNR comparison table between R2D2/AIRI/CLEAN is given in this piece — R2D2 (Aghabiglou et al. 2024, ApJS 273, 3) and fast-resolve (Roth et al. 2024) are cited specifically as AI/Bayesian methods that adopt CLEAN-like major/minor loop structures "for efficiency," implying scalability is a recognized design constraint even for these ML methods.
- **AI-assisted hybrid result (Fiedler et al. 2025, Fig. 4):** a network trained on single-scale vs. MS-CLEAN component pairs transforms Högbom CLEAN output into a multiscale-equivalent reconstruction, reportedly "achiev[ing] the accuracy of [MS-CLEAN] with minimal computational overhead and bias from the training set" — demonstrated visually on an ENZO simulated sky (ground truth vs. Högbom model vs. network+Högbom model).
- **Data-volume/dynamic-range framing:** ngVLA, SKA, and ALMA-WSU are said to produce "data volumes and dynamic ranges that exceed the scalability of traditional methods," though no specific TB/PB or dynamic-range target numbers are given beyond the compute-flop figures above.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
Very high relevance: this is a forward-looking synthesis piece that explicitly frames gridding as the dominant computational bottleneck for next-generation arrays and positions AI-based deconvolution methods (R2D2 and peers) — alongside Bayesian inference and CLEAN-hybrids — as the most promising long-term path for a pipeline that must scale to ngVLA/SKA data volumes, directly supporting the case for ML-based gridding/deconvolution research in this UROP project.
