# Scalable Non-Cartesian Magnetic Resonance Imaging with R2D2

**Bibcode:** 2024arXiv240317905C
**Authors:** Yiwei Chen, Chao Tang, Amir Aghabiglou, Chung San Chu, Yves Wiaux (Institute of Sensors, Signals and Systems, Heriot-Watt University, Edinburgh; Chao Tang also at EPCC, University of Edinburgh)
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240317905C/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17905

## One-line summary
The authors port the R2D2 "series of residual images" DNN paradigm from radio-interferometric imaging to non-Cartesian (radial) single-coil MRI reconstruction, comparing it against unrolled networks and a Plug-and-Play (PnP) baseline in terms of reconstruction quality, scalability, and speed.

## Method
R2D2 reconstructs an image as a sequence of residual estimates: at each iteration i, a U-Net-based DNN takes the current image estimate x^(i-1) and its back-projected data residual r^(i-1) (the "data-consistency term", analogous to computing a dirty-image-style residual via the adjoint measurement operator) and outputs an additive correction, x^i = x^(i-1) + G_θi(r^(i-1), x^(i-1)). Networks are trained sequentially with an L1 loss, with the measurement operator (NUFFT, i.e. interpolation + FFT + zero-padding for non-Cartesian k-space) kept outside the network (unlike unrolled architectures), which is what gives R2D2 its scalability. They also propose two unrolled variants for comparison: R2D2-Net (NUFFT), which embeds the exact NUFFT operator in data-consistency layers (accurate but not scalable), and R2D2-Net (FFT), which substitutes a scalable FFT-based approximation of the point-spread-function convolution for data consistency. Training data: denoised magnitude knee images (320×320) from the FastMRI single-coil dataset (25,743 train / 6,605 validation), with simulated radial k-space sampling (golden-angle spokes), density compensation, and additive Gaussian noise calibrated to a target dynamic range. Acceleration Factor (AF) and number of spokes are varied (Ns from 8–80, i.e. AF 4–40) during training so the model generalizes across undersampling levels. Baselines compared: vanilla U-Net (end-to-end, no data-consistency layer), NC-PDNet (unrolled, non-scalable, embeds NUFFT), and AIRI (an advanced PnP algorithm previously introduced in radio-interferometric imaging, using a shelf of denoisers with non-expansiveness constraints).

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **SNR (Signal-to-Noise Ratio, dB):** SNR(x, x̄) = 20·log10(‖x̄‖₂ / ‖x̄ − x‖₂), the standard L2-norm-based fidelity metric relative to ground truth x̄. Primary metric for overall reconstruction quality.
- **logSNR:** SNR computed after applying a logarithmic intensity mapping rlog(x) = log_a(a·x + 1) (with a set to the image's Dynamic Range) to both reconstruction and ground truth before computing SNR — used specifically to assess recovery of faint/low-intensity signal (i.e. high-dynamic-range fidelity), directly analogous to the "high-dynamic-range imaging" emphasis of the original R2D2 radio-astronomy papers.
- **Dynamic Range (DR):** defined as ratio of max to faintest recoverable intensity (reciprocal of background noise std σ); DR spanned 10–10⁴ across the simulated test set to stress high-dynamic-range performance.
- **Acceleration Factor (AF)** and **number of spokes (Ns):** AF = √N / Ns; results (SNR/logSNR) reported as a function of AF (spokes swept 10–80) to test robustness to undersampling — a direct analogue of visibility-sampling sparsity in radio interferometry.
- **Number of iterations (DNN series length):** SNR/logSNR reported vs. number of R2D2 iterations (1–8), showing performance rises and converges; R2D2 needs only ~8 networks vs. AIRI's PnP iteration count.
- **Additional Acceleration Ratio (Table I):** AF_R2D2 / AF_Algo at matched SNR (16, 21, 26 dB) — quantifies how much faster (in terms of allowable undersampling / scan time) R2D2 reaches a given quality vs. other methods. E.g. at SNR=16, R2D2 tolerates ~1.92× the undersampling of U-Net, ~1.69× AIRI, ~1.15× R2D2-Net (FFT), ~1.54× NC-PDNet.
- **Runtime / compute cost (Table II):** number of trainable parameters (M), Training Time (TT, hours), Inference Time (IT, seconds ± std), and iteration count, averaged over 160 test inverse problems:
  - AIRI: 0.6M params, 48h TT, 1857±387 s IT, 616±138 iterations (non-scalable PnP).
  - U-Net: 31M, 52h, 0.053±0.007 s, 1 iteration.
  - R2D2-Net (FFT): 248M, 152h, 0.129±0.004 s, 1 iteration (scalable).
  - R2D2: 248M, 140h, 0.237±0.005 s, 8 iterations (scalable).
  - NC-PDNet: 1.6M, 230h, 0.263±0.011 s, 1 iteration (non-scalable).
  - R2D2-Net (NUFFT): 248M, 315h, 0.237±0.005 s, 1 iteration (non-scalable, treated as an "oracle" upper bound).
  - Headline result: R2D2's inference is roughly four orders of magnitude faster than AIRI, and R2D2-Net (NUFFT) costs ~163 extra training hours vs. R2D2-Net (FFT) purely from NUFFT computation.
- **Qualitative/visual comparison (Fig. 2):** reconstructed images and GT-difference maps for one validation sample at DR=167, AF=16, with (SNR, logSNR) shown per method: AIRI (15.55, 18.96), U-Net (14.93, 17.88), NC-PDNet (14.40, 17.65), R2D2-Net (FFT) (17.46, 22.85), R2D2 (18.44, 23.15), R2D2-Net (NUFFT) (19.39, 25.10) — showing R2D2 and R2D2-Net (NUFFT) give visually near-identical, best reconstructions.

**Overall ranking (quality):** R2D2-Net (NUFFT) > R2D2 > R2D2-Net (FFT) > AIRI/NC-PDNet/U-Net (roughly), with R2D2 explicitly reported as (i) suboptimal vs. its own non-scalable unrolled NUFFT variant, but (ii) superior to the scalable FFT-approximation unrolled variant, and (iii) superior to the PnP baseline (AIRI) while needing far fewer iterations (8 vs. ~616) and far less inference time.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
This paper is a direct cross-domain validation of the R2D2 "series of residual DNNs using back-projected data residual + image estimate" paradigm applied to non-Cartesian Fourier sampling (radial k-space) rather than radio-interferometric visibilities — the back-projection/data-consistency term here (r = x_d − κ·Re{Φ†Φx}) plays exactly the role of a dirty-image residual computed via gridding+iFFT in the radio pipeline, and the NUFFT operator Φ (interpolation U, FFT F, zero-padding Z) is structurally identical to the gridding+iFFT step. Their explicit scalability comparison (few-iteration R2D2 vs. many-iteration PnP vs. NUFFT-embedding unrolled nets) and use of SNR/logSNR/dynamic-range/acceleration-factor robustness metrics offers a template for evaluating ML-based gridding/deconvolution replacements in the visibility pipeline, particularly for quantifying performance-vs-scalability and performance-vs-undersampling (acceleration factor ≈ visibility sparsity) trade-offs.
