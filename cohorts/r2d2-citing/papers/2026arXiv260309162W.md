# POLISH'ing the Sky: Wide-Field and High-Dynamic Range Interferometric Image Reconstruction with Application to Strong Lens Discovery

**Bibcode:** 2026arXiv260309162W
**Authors:** Zihui Wu, Liam Connor, Samuel McCarty, Katherine L. Bouman
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260309162W/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09162 (arXiv:2603.09162v1 [astro-ph.IM], submitted 10 Mar 2026)

## One-line summary
The authors extend the POLISH deep-learning image-plane deconvolution/super-resolution framework (Connor et al. 2022) with patch-wise training/stitching (for scaling to wide fields of view up to 12,960×12,960 px) and an arcsinh nonlinear intensity transform (for handling dynamic ranges up to ~10^6), producing POLISH+/POLISH++, and show these can super-resolve strong gravitational lens systems near/below the PSF scale, potentially yielding ~10× more DSA galaxy-galaxy lenses than image-plane CLEAN.

## Method
POLISH is a CNN-based super-resolution/deconvolution model that learns to map a "dirty" (PSF-convolved, downsampled) interferometric image to a high-resolution ground-truth sky image, operating entirely in the image plane. This paper's contributions on top of the base POLISH architecture are: (1) a patch-wise training and inference/stitching strategy so the model can be trained on 324×324 px patches (28,800 non-overlapping patches per training image, ~6M detectable galaxy examples) and reconstruct arbitrarily large wide-field images without cross-patch contamination artifacts; and (2) an AsinhStretch-based nonlinear transform applied to pixel intensities before training to compress high dynamic range (reducing max dynamic range by over an order of magnitude) so the network isn't dominated by a few very bright sources. Three models are compared: baseline POLISH, POLISH+ (patch-wise only), and POLISH++ (patch-wise + arcsinh transform). Evaluation uses the T-RECS simulation suite (realistic radio sky model, dynamic range 10^4–10^6) convolved with a physically realistic DSA (Deep Synoptic Array) PSF; CLEAN images (CASA `deconvolve` task, Högbom minor cycle, loop gain 0.1) serve as the classical baseline. Source detection uses the SEP package; strong-lens simulations use T-RECS sources + SL-Hammocks halo-based lens models + GLAFIC ray-tracing, with a Structure-1 CNN lens finder (Rezaei et al. 2022) trained separately per image type (ground truth, dirty, CLEAN, POLISH, POLISH++). A model-mismatch/robustness study perturbs the PSF with warping distortions (γ ∈ [0,30]) not seen during training.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **Precision, Recall, F1 score (source detection, via SEP):** Precision = TP/(TP+FP), Recall = TP/(TP+FN), F1 = 2·TP/(2·TP+FP+FN), where a true positive is a ground-truth galaxy matched to a reconstructed detection within a size-dependent pixel threshold. Results (Table 3): CLEAN 0.3612/0.2220/0.2750; POLISH 0.5560/0.4612/0.5042; POLISH+ 0.8744/0.5751/0.6938; POLISH++ 0.8433/0.6142/0.7107. POLISH+/POLISH++ substantially outperform CLEAN and baseline POLISH on all three metrics; POLISH++ improves recall by ~4% over POLISH+ via better precision/recall balance. Performance is also tracked as a function of SNR threshold (3 to 300,000; Fig. 4), with POLISH+/++ showing the largest gains over CLEAN at low SNR (the majority regime).
- **Shape & flux estimation accuracy — RMSE on true positives:** Major-axis FWHM (θ_A), minor-axis FWHM (θ_B), and flux (Jy/pixel), computed from SEP-returned parameters on true-positive detections (SNR>300 subset for scatter plots). Results (Table 3): θ_A RMSE — CLEAN 1.0046″, POLISH 0.9642″, POLISH+ 0.4335″, POLISH++ 0.4654″; θ_B RMSE — CLEAN 0.7862″, POLISH 0.3219″, POLISH+ 0.1889″, POLISH++ 0.2056″; flux RMSE — CLEAN 3.2625×10⁻⁴ Jy/px (best/lowest), POLISH 1.9504×10⁻³, POLISH+ 3.8411×10⁻³, POLISH++ 3.1703×10⁻³ (CLEAN remains superior for flux fidelity — attributed to the nonlinear learned reconstruction lacking explicit flux calibration, whereas CLEAN preserves absolute flux scaling). CLEAN's shape/resolving power asymptotes to the PSF FWHM (≈3.3″), while POLISH/++ achieve true super-resolution below this scale.
- **Dynamic range** (qualitative/comparative, not a single scalar score): defined as ratio of brightest to dimmest recoverable pixel; used to characterize both the T-RECS test data (10^4–10^6) and to benchmark prior DL methods in Table 1 (POLISH ~10², Radionets ~5, Deflation Net ~10², R2D2 ~5×10^5, GU-Net/RI-GAN ~6×10², POLISH++ ~10^6 — the highest reported); CLEAN's practical dynamic range is noted as "several hundred" before the Högbom loop diverges.
- **Strong-lens discovery — True Positive Rate (TPR) at fixed False Positive Rate (FPR=10⁻³):** a CNN lens finder (Rezaei et al. 2022 architecture) is trained/evaluated separately per image type; TPR vs. Einstein radius is reported (Fig. 7). CLEAN-trained finder performance drops off below the ~3×PSF-FWHM image-separation limit found by Rezaei et al. (2022), consistent with prior literature. POLISH/POLISH++-trained finders recover lenses down to near the PSF FWHM itself (a further ~1× reduction in the minimum separation), performance close to the "true sky" upper bound — translating to an estimated ~10× (order-of-magnitude) increase in the number of DSA-discoverable galaxy-galaxy lenses relative to CLEAN-only search (per McCarty & Connor 2025 yield estimates). No absolute completeness/purity numbers are given beyond this TPR-vs-Einstein-radius curve at fixed FPR.
- **PSNR (peak signal-to-noise ratio):** used specifically in the model-mismatch/robustness experiment (Fig. 8) to quantify reconstruction quality degradation as the PSF is perturbed by warping distortions γ ∈ [0,30] outside the training distribution (model trained only at γ=0). PSNR degrades predictably with increasing γ, but the authors note this pixel-level metric is more sensitive to model mismatch than actual visual/qualitative reconstruction quality, which remains comparatively stable even at extreme distortion (γ=30) — flagged as a caveat about PSNR's limits as a fidelity proxy under calibration/model-mismatch conditions.
- **Fine-tuning convergence speed** (efficiency metric, not fidelity per se): under PSF mismatch, fine-tuning an existing POLISH++ model reaches peak PSNR in 11 epochs vs. 57 epochs training from scratch (>5× speedup), both far outperforming the unadapted mismatched baseline.
- **Comparisons to R2D2 and other DL methods:** R2D2 (Aghabiglou et al. 2024) and other prior DL imaging methods (Radionets, Deflation Net, GU-Net, RI-GAN) are compared only via the qualitative capability table (Table 1: max image size, max dynamic range, domain, training data, model-mismatch handling) — POLISH++ is reported as the only method combining large image size (12,960 px vs. 512 px for R2D2), the highest dynamic range (~10^6 vs. 5×10^5 for R2D2), non-Gaussian source morphology (strong lenses), and synthetic PSF model-mismatch testing. No direct head-to-head SNR/PSNR/SSIM benchmark against R2D2 is run in this paper; CLEAN and prior POLISH are the only methods directly re-evaluated on the same test data.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
This paper operates purely on the image-plane deconvolution/super-resolution step (dirty image → clean image), directly relevant to the deconvolution stage of the pipeline, and offers a concrete evaluation methodology — detection precision/recall/F1, shape/flux RMSE, dynamic-range handling, and downstream science-metric (lens TPR) — that could be adapted to benchmark ML deconvolution modules; its patch-wise training/stitching and dynamic-range-compression (arcsinh) techniques are also directly transferable to any CNN-based deconvolution or gridding-correction network needing to scale to large, high-dynamic-range wide-field images.
