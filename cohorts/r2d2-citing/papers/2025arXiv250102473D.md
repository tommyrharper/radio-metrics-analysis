# IRIS: A Bayesian Approach for Image Reconstruction in Radio Interferometry with expressive Score-Based priors

**Bibcode:** 2025arXiv250102473D
**Authors:** Noé Dia, M. J. Yantovski-Barth, Alexandre Adam, Micah Bowles, Laurence Perreault-Levasseur, Yashar Hezaveh, Anna Scaife
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250102473D/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02473

## One-line summary
IRIS uses a score-based diffusion model trained on optical galaxy images as an expressive prior, combined with a Gaussian likelihood in uv-space, to generate calibrated Bayesian posterior samples for radio-interferometric image reconstruction, applied to ALMA/DSHARP protoplanetary disk data.

## Method
IRIS trains score-based generative models (SBMs, both VE and VP SDE formulations) on optical galaxy image datasets (PROBES, SKIRT) and uses them as priors within a posterior-sampling framework for interferometric imaging. The likelihood is a Gaussian model in uv (visibility) space, with a "Convolved Likelihood Approximation" used at higher noise. Posterior samples are drawn via predictor-corrector (PC) and Euler SDE samplers. The method is validated on synthetic simulations and then applied to real ALMA DSHARP survey visibilities of protoplanetary disks (e.g. RU Lup, WaOph 6), deliberately testing robustness under a misspecified prior (galaxy images used to reconstruct disks).

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **TARP (Tests of Accuracy with Random Points)**: primary calibration/coverage metric estimating whether posterior samples are statistically calibrated, using 500 synthetic observations x 500 posterior samples (250,000 total). VP SDE + PC sampling achieved calibrated posteriors with tuned parameters; VE SDE could not achieve calibration; Euler-only sampling showed significant bias.
- **Chi-squared (χ²) goodness-of-fit**: computed on residuals between predicted and observed visibilities in Fourier space. Key finding: "calibrated posterior samples do not necessarily achieve the best residuals" — on real DSHARP data, χ² values were much higher than degrees of freedom, attributed to modeling only small image regions while assuming zero flux elsewhere.
- **Qualitative dynamic range / resolution comparison to baselines (Fig. 7)**: compared against CLEAN (Högbom/Cornwell variants) and MPoL (regularized maximum likelihood). IRIS reports "competitive resolution and dynamic range performance," recovers the same substructures (crescents, gaps, rings) as CLEAN/MPoL, and avoids dirty-image artifacts persistent in CLEAN reconstructions.
- **No standard PSNR/SSIM/SNR metrics are reported** — the paper explicitly does not benchmark against pixel-fidelity metrics like PSNR or SSIM, nor does it compare to MCMC-based UQ methods like QuantifAI; it relies on TARP calibration and χ² residuals instead.
- **Uncertainty quantification**: posterior samples yield pixel-wise 16th–84th percentile ranges and per-pixel statistical moments, providing explicit UQ absent from deterministic methods (CLEAN/MPoL).
- **Computational cost (reported as a trade-off, not a fidelity metric)**: 250 posterior samples per disk generated in ~2.5 hours on 10 V100 GPUs; 0.72 A100 GPU-years used for full calibration/simulation testing; explicitly noted to be much slower than CLEAN/MPoL.
- **Prior robustness check**: comparing reconstructions from three priors (VE/VP-PROBES, VP-SKIRT) on RU Lup showed consistent recovery of main disk features despite prior misspecification.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
IRIS replaces the classical CLEAN deconvolution step with a learned score-based prior operating directly on uv-space visibilities via posterior sampling, offering a template for injecting deep generative priors into the deconvolution/image-formation stage while natively producing calibrated uncertainty estimates — directly relevant to ML-based alternatives to CLEAN in the pipeline being studied.
