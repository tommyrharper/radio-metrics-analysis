# The Role of Artificial Intelligence in the SKA Era

**Bibcode:** 2026arXiv260628493D
**Authors:** Philipp Denzel, Frank-Peter Schilling, Elena Gavagnin
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260628493D/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28493

## One-line summary
A broad review (chapter for "Advancing Astrophysics with the SKA II", 2026) surveying how AI/ML — deep learning, generative models, reinforcement learning, and federated learning — can address the data-scale and analysis challenges of the SKA, covering source detection, RFI mitigation, calibration, imaging, and parameter inference.

## Method
This is a survey/review chapter, not a novel-methods paper. It summarizes AI applications across the SKA science pipeline (detection, anomaly detection, calibration, imaging, scheduling, system control), emphasizing SKA's petabyte-scale datasets and terabit-per-second data streams that strain traditional analysis paradigms. It explicitly discusses replacements for the CLEAN deconvolution algorithm, noting CLEAN's "poor computational scalability with image size and source count, dependence on manual expert intervention for convergence, [and] reduced accuracy for faint or diffuse structures." It reviews several imaging paradigms: AIRI (neural denoiser within an optimization framework), direct U-Net visibility-to-image models, POLISH (ResNet-based dirty-image super-resolution), R2D2 (iteration-specific neural networks replacing CLEAN's heuristic minor-cycle steps), VisRec (semi-supervised visibility reconstruction), and conditional diffusion models for source localization/flux estimation. It also stresses the need for explainability, uncertainty quantification, and physics-informed inductive biases to preserve scientific rigor.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **SNR improvement** — POLISH reports a 3 dB SNR gain over CLEAN dirty images (super-resolution/denoising context).
- **Dynamic range / imaging fidelity (qualitative)** — R2D2 is described as delivering "high-dynamic-range imaging at CLEAN-like speeds," and AIRI as achieving "image quality comparable to SARA at significantly reduced computational cost" (comparisons framed relative to existing optimization-based imagers rather than a single unified quantitative metric).
- **Computational scalability / speed** — Inference speed and computational cost are repeatedly used as comparison axes (e.g., R2D2 vs. CLEAN speed; AIRI cost vs. SARA; U-Net "extreme inference speed" traded against hallucination risk); relevant given SKA's petabyte/terabit-scale throughput requirements.
- **Detection accuracy** — true-positive/false-positive rates cited for CNN-based source (lens) finding (95.3% TPR at 0.008% FPR), illustrative of classification-style metrics used elsewhere in the pipeline (not imaging-specific but part of the same evaluation culture).
- **Calibration accuracy thresholds** — errors must stay below ~0.001% to avoid foreground contamination, relevant to ML calibration steps upstream of imaging.
- **Qualitative/robustness metrics** — risk of "hallucinated structures and bias propagation from training data" and lack of "statistically calibrated confidence measures" are flagged as open evaluation gaps for deep-learning imaging methods, implicitly calling for uncertainty quantification as a future fidelity metric.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
Directly relevant: the review explicitly discusses R2D2, AIRI, POLISH, and direct visibility-to-image U-Nets as ML alternatives to CLEAN-style deconvolution, and frames SKA-scale computational scalability and hallucination/uncertainty-quantification risk as the key open evaluation criteria for any ML imaging replacement in this pipeline.
