# HyperAIRI: A Plug-and-play Algorithm for Precise Hyperspectral Image Reconstruction in Radio Interferometry

**Bibcode:** 2026ApJS..283....9T
**Authors:** Chao Tang (1,2), Arwa Dabbech (1), Adrian Jackson (2), Yves Wiaux (1) — (1) Institute of Sensors, Signals and Systems, Heriot-Watt University, Edinburgh, UK; (2) EPCC, University of Edinburgh, UK
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026ApJS..283....9T/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15198 (HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15198v2)

## One-line summary
HyperAIRI extends the AIRI plug-and-play imaging paradigm from monochromatic to hyperspectral (wideband) radio-interferometric imaging, using learned denoisers coupled across spectral channels via a power-law spectral model, and shows improved reconstruction fidelity and spectral accuracy over monochromatic AIRI/uSARA and WSClean at comparable or lower cost.

## Method
HyperAIRI applies a forward-backward (plug-and-play) algorithmic structure in which, at each iteration, a data-fidelity gradient step is taken per spectral channel and a *learned hyperspectral denoiser* is applied jointly across channels, using side information from neighbouring channels and enforcing a physical power-law spectral model to couple channels together (with periodic global updates of the spectral index map). Denoisers are dynamic-range-matched and Jacobian-regularized for convergence guarantees, and spatial image faceting is used for scalability to large image sizes. The paper also introduces **Hyper-uSARA**, an optimization-based (non-learned) counterpart using the same power-law spectral coupling, as an additional baseline/ablation. The design requires only local (per-channel) communication at each iteration, with global communication needed only for spectral-index updates, making the algorithm scalable on distributed/HPC + GPU systems. Methods are validated on simulated wideband VLA observations and on real data.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **SNR (signal-to-noise ratio, dB):** SNR(X, X̄) = 20·log₁₀(‖X̄‖_F / ‖X̄ − X‖_F), the standard linear-scale image-fidelity metric relative to ground truth.
- **logSNR (dB):** SNR computed after a per-image logarithmic intensity transform, r_log(X) = max(X)·log_a(a·X/max(X) + 1), which emphasizes faint/low-intensity emission relative to the target dynamic range a. Used to assess recovery of faint structure across channels.
- **RDR (residual-to-dirty ratio, ×10⁻³):** RDR = ‖X^res‖_F / ‖X^dirty‖_F, where X^res = X^dirty − Re{Φ†(Φ(X))} is the residual dirty image. An image-domain **data-fidelity** metric (lower = residual closer to noise-only, i.e. better fit to the visibility data).
- **sSNR (spectral-index SNR, dB):** SNR between the reconstructed and ground-truth spectral index maps (estimated via linear regression across channels), quantifying accuracy of the recovered spectral behaviour — the key metric specific to the hyperspectral setting.
- **Computational cost:** number of series iterations, total wall-clock reconstruction time (hours), and per-step timing breakdown (e.g., HyperAIRI: gradient step ≈1.61±0.23 s, denoising step ≈0.52±0.25 s).

**Reported results (simulated VLA benchmark, Table 1, mean ± std):**

| Algorithm | SNR (dB) | logSNR (dB) | RDR (×10⁻³) | sSNR (dB) | Iterations | Time (hr) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WSClean | 11.27 ± 1.57 | 12.05 ± 4.08 | 2.36 ± 1.27 | 3.81 ± 5.91 | 10 ± 1 | 0.56 ± 0.24 |
| uSARA | 28.32 ± 3.24 | 21.17 ± 3.56 | 2.27 ± 1.32 | 9.54 ± 2.34 | 1251 ± 299 | 2.60 ± 0.55 |
| AIRI | 28.70 ± 2.36 | 20.67 ± 5.01 | 2.62 ± 1.41 | 9.18 ± 3.90 | 3000 ± 0 | 1.84 ± 0.14 |
| Hyper-uSARA | 29.30 ± 2.91 | 22.30 ± 4.15 | 2.32 ± 1.34 | **11.35 ± 3.78** | 1827 ± 675 | 2.42 ± 0.87 |
| **HyperAIRI** | **30.53 ± 1.87** | **23.07 ± 5.13** | 2.44 ± 1.36 | 10.87 ± 3.91 | 3000 ± 0 | 1.90 ± 0.14 |

- **Comparison to monochromatic baselines:** Hyper-uSARA improves mean SNR over uSARA by ~1 dB; HyperAIRI improves SNR over AIRI by ~1.8 dB and logSNR by ~2.4 dB (Hyper-uSARA gains ~1.1 dB logSNR over uSARA). The hyperspectral (power-law-coupled) versions consistently beat their monochromatic counterparts on SNR/logSNR/sSNR.
- **Comparison to WSClean:** WSClean is by far the lowest-fidelity method (SNR 11.27 dB vs. HyperAIRI's 30.53 dB; sSNR only 3.81 dB), though it is fastest (0.56 hr) and has comparable RDR/data-fidelity to the other methods.
- **Data fidelity (RDR):** all methods achieve broadly comparable RDR (~2.3–2.6×10⁻³), close to the ground-truth reference RDR of (2.46 ± 1.37)×10⁻³, i.e. HyperAIRI achieves competitive data fidelity to WSClean and the optimization-based methods despite far higher image-domain SNR.
- **Spectral accuracy (sSNR):** Hyper-uSARA attains the best sSNR (11.35 dB), HyperAIRI close behind (10.87 dB); both far exceed monochromatic AIRI/uSARA (~9 dB) and WSClean (3.81 dB).
- **Compute cost:** despite needing more iterations than pure optimization methods (fixed at 3000 for AIRI/HyperAIRI), plug-and-play (denoiser-based) methods remain faster overall than optimization-based Hyper-uSARA/uSARA due to efficient GPU-based denoiser inference; HyperAIRI (1.90 hr) is faster than Hyper-uSARA (2.42 hr) and uSARA (2.60 hr), though still slower than WSClean.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
HyperAIRI is a direct extension of the plug-and-play/learned-denoiser approach (as in AIRI/R2D2) to the multi-channel (wideband, hyperspectral) case, showing how a learned deconvolution step applied per-channel to the gridded/iFFT'd dirty image, with spectral coupling via a power-law model, can improve both spatial fidelity (SNR/logSNR) and spectral fidelity (sSNR) relative to single-channel methods — directly relevant if the UROP pipeline needs to handle multi-frequency data or wants a benchmark suite (SNR, logSNR, RDR, sSNR, iteration/runtime cost) for evaluating ML-based deconvolution modules.
