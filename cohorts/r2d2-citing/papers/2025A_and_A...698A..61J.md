# Deep learning inference with the Event Horizon Telescope: II. The ZINGULARITY framework for Bayesian artificial neural networks

**Bibcode:** 2025A&A...698A..61J
**Authors:** M. Janssen, C.-k. Chan, J. Davelaar, M. Wielgus
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A%26A...698A..61J/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13875 (arXiv:2506.13875v1 [astro-ph.IM])

## One-line summary
ZINGULARITY is an open-source TensorFlow/TensorFlow-Probability framework of Bayesian neural networks (ResNet + DenseVariational layers) that infers GRMHD accretion/black-hole parameters (spin, MAD/SANE state, R_high, inclination, position angle) directly from full-polarization EHT visibilities, propagating both epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty and using a bootstrapping scheme over calibration/thermal-noise errors to produce conservative posterior parameter estimates.

## Method
The network takes 8-channel full-polarization visibility data (real/imag of RR, RL, LR, LL) as input and regresses/classifies GRMHD-GRRT model parameters. It combines ResNet convolutional layers for feature extraction with DenseVariational fully-connected layers that place trainable Normal surrogate posteriors on weights, giving a variational Bayesian neural network (BANN) rather than a point-estimate network. Training used ~600,000 synthetic M87* samples and ~252,000 synthetic Sgr A* samples generated with the SYMBA synthetic-data pipeline (realistic EHT signal-path corruptions: gains, leakage, thermal noise). At inference time, uncertainty is quantified two ways: (1) intrinsic Bayesian weight-distribution sampling (epistemic + aleatoric), and (2) a bootstrapping procedure that resamples the observed data over known calibration uncertainties (per-antenna gain scatter, calibrator uncertainty, gain-curve fits, per-baseline thermal noise) and draws many posterior samples per resampled dataset to obtain conservative, marginalized parameter posteriors.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **Mean absolute error (MAE):** used to track regression performance (spin, R_high, inclination, position angle) on training vs. validation data across epochs; reported qualitatively via validation curves (Fig. 4) rather than single summary numbers in the extracted text.
- **Classification accuracy:** fraction of correct predictions over total predictions, used for the discrete MAD/SANE magnetic-state classification; reported as "near-perfect" for the M87* fiducial model.
- **Validation-error comparison across targets:** Sgr A* models show higher validation errors than M87* (attributed to ~2.4x fewer training samples and greater intrinsic image variability); R_high shows the highest validation error of all regressed parameters (attributed to weak influence on GRRT image morphology for MAD models).
- **Bayesian uncertainty quantification:** epistemic (model) and aleatoric (data) uncertainty captured via DenseVariational layer posteriors; no single scalar "credible interval coverage %" or calibration-plot statistic was found in the extracted text, but the paper explicitly frames the goal as obtaining "trustworthy uncertainties" and using them to flag failure modes on uncharacterizable data.
- **Bootstrapping over calibration uncertainty:** ~100 posterior samples drawn per bootstrapped dataset (~100 resampled datasets) to fold gain/leakage/thermal-noise systematics into final parameter posteriors; a forward pass takes only a few seconds, so this Monte Carlo procedure is computationally cheap.
- **Robustness/ablation checks (used as fidelity diagnostics rather than single metrics):** performance degrades sharply for Sgr A* without polarization info or with Stokes-I-only input; validation error is artificially "strongly reduced" (i.e., misleadingly optimistic) when only thermal noise is modeled instead of the full SYMBA signal-path corruption, flagged as an overfitting/oversimplification risk; train/validation split by GRMHD time-frame (first 50% vs. last 50%) gives errors comparable to a random shuffle split, supporting generalization beyond specific snapshots; architecture/hyperparameter variations (activation, optimizer, regularization, depth) leave performance largely unaffected.
- **Compute/runtime cost:** one training epoch over 600,000 samples (21,956 visibility points each) takes ~30 s on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU; obtaining 100 posterior samples from 100 bootstrapped datasets takes ~20 s; post-training inference is "practically instantaneous"; representative BANN has ~12M trainable parameters; distributed training via Horovod scales efficiently over native TensorFlow up to 128 GPUs.
- **Comparison to baselines:** no explicit quantitative side-by-side metrics against other EHT imaging pipelines (eht-imaging, SMILI) or other Bayesian/NN inference methods were found. Comparison is qualitative: contrasted with traditional χ²-based GRMHD model scoring (which breaks down in low-SNR regimes where errors become non-Gaussian and gain-dependent) and with prior (non-Bayesian, non-polarimetric, smaller-dataset) EHT neural-network parameter-inference work, over which this paper claims improvement via larger training sets, inclusion of Sgr A*, fuller signal-path modeling, polarization information, and Bayesian/bootstrap uncertainty quantification.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
ZINGULARITY operates entirely in the visibility domain rather than through gridding/iFFT/deconvolution, but it is directly relevant as a worked example of a variational Bayesian neural network (DenseVariational layers) that produces calibrated, propagated uncertainty on outputs from raw interferometric data — a template the UROP project could adapt to attach epistemic/aleatoric uncertainty estimates to an ML-based gridding, iFFT, or deconvolution stage rather than only to downstream physical-parameter inference.
