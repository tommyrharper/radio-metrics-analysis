# The AIRI plug-and-play algorithm for image reconstruction in radio-interferometry: variations and robustness

**Bibcode:** 2025MNRAS.537.1608T
**Authors:** Matthieu Terris, Chao Tang, Adrian Jackson, Yves Wiaux
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.537.1608T/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07137 (arXiv:2312.07137v3 [eess.IV])

## One-line summary
This paper extends the AIRI (AI for Regularisation in radio-Interferometric imaging) plug-and-play algorithm — which replaces a handcrafted proximal-operator prior with a learned DNN denoiser inside an optimisation-algorithm backbone — by plugging AIRI denoisers into multiple algorithmic backbones (not just forward-backward, but also primal-dual forward-backward to instantiate a PnP counterpart of the constrained SARA algorithm), and demonstrates that AIRI's reconstruction quality is robust both to the choice of backbone and to radical changes in the DNN denoiser's training data (astronomy vs. medical MRI images), while also introducing an uncertainty-quantification functionality based on ensembles of independently trained denoisers.

## Method
AIRI is a plug-and-play (PnP) image reconstruction algorithm for radio interferometry that replaces the proximal operator of a handcrafted image prior (as used in optimisation methods like SARA/uSARA) with a DNN denoiser acting as an implicit regulariser, iterated inside a forward-backward (FB) algorithmic loop alternating a data-fidelity gradient step (via the measurement operator Φ, i.e. degridding/gridding + FFT) and a denoising ("regularisation") step. This paper's contributions are: (i) showing AIRI denoisers can be plugged unaltered into other algorithmic backbones, in particular a primal-dual forward-backward (PDFB) scheme to build a PnP counterpart of the constrained SARA algorithm itself (yielding variants such as "cAIRI"); (ii) testing robustness of AIRI reconstruction quality to strong changes in the denoiser's training dataset, comparing denoisers trained on 5000 optical astronomical images (OAID) versus 5000 MRI knee images (MRID); (iii) developing an epistemic-uncertainty quantification method by training K=15 denoiser realisations (varying random initialisation/training) and computing ensemble statistics of the resulting reconstructions. Validation uses simulated MeerKAT-type observations of three 512×512 radio images (3C353, Hercules A, Centaurus A) across 5 pointing directions and 4 observation durations (ΔT = 1, 2, 4, 8 h), giving 60 synthetic inverse problems spanning dynamic ranges from ~2.9×10³ (ΔT=1h) to ~1.1×10⁴ (ΔT=8h), plus qualitative tests on real data.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **SNR (signal-to-noise ratio, dB):** SNR(x̄, x̂) = 20·log₁₀(‖x̄‖ / ‖x̄ − x̂‖), between ground truth x̄ and estimate x̂; the standard overall image-fidelity metric across the full dynamic range.
- **logSNR (dB):** SNR computed after a logarithmic pixel-wise transform, logSNR(x̄, x̂) = SNR(rlog_a(x̄), rlog_a(x̂)) with rlog_a(x) = x_max·log_a(a·x/x_max + 1); designed to emphasise faint/low-intensity features that linear SNR under-weights. The transform parameter was set to a = 2.5×10³, the lowest dynamic range among the simulated observations, to keep comparisons consistent.
- **Dynamic range (DR):** ratio between brightest and faintest image features; test/ground-truth images had DR slightly above 10⁴, and simulated observations had average DR per integration time ranging from 2.9×10³ (ΔT=1h) to 1.1×10⁴ (ΔT=8h).
- **Residual/data-fidelity assessment:** qualitative inspection of residual dirty images, Re{Φ†(y − Φx)}, to check consistency between the reconstruction and the measured visibilities (no residual structure = good data fit).
- **Robustness / epistemic uncertainty quantification:** using K=15 independently trained denoiser realisations, they compute the empirical std across reconstructions, std(Sol) = sqrt(Σ(x̂_k − mean(Sol))²/(K−1)), and a relative/normalised version std(Sol)/mean(Sol), to visualise per-pixel model uncertainty; SNR/logSNR varied by about 1.5 dB (SNR) and 4 dB (logSNR) across the 15 realisations, i.e. relatively small variation given the underlying stochasticity of training — evidence of robustness to training randomness.
- **Cross-domain training-data robustness:** denoisers trained on MRI images (MRID) achieved SNR/logSNR comparable to those trained on optical astronomical images (OAID), with only minor visual differences (e.g. in point-source recovery), supporting the claim that AIRI's learned regularisation is not narrowly tied to matching the target imaging domain.
- **Runtime / compute cost (per denoising step, mean ± std, seconds):** AIRI on GPU: 0.05 ± 0.02 s; AIRI on CPU: 7.92 ± 0.52 s; SARA (proximal operator, presumably CPU): 1.31 ± 1.12 s; BM3D: 15.08 ± 0.40 s. On GPU, AIRI denoisers give roughly a 20-fold speed-up over the proximity operators used in SARA/uSARA.
- **Comparison to baselines (SNR/logSNR):** across observation times ΔT ∈ {1h, 2h, 4h, 8h}, AIRI and its constrained variant cAIRI improve by 1–2 dB in both SNR and logSNR over their pure optimisation counterparts uSARA and SARA respectively; constrained variants (cAIRI, SARA) generally outperform unconstrained ones (AIRI, uSARA), particularly on SNR. Example at ΔT=8h (SNR, logSNR in dB): MS-CLEAN (5.45, 7.07), SARA (27.74, 25.16), cAIRI-OAID (28.65, 26.29) — i.e. AIRI-family methods dramatically outperform CLEAN (>20 dB) and modestly outperform their optimisation-based (SARA/uSARA) counterparts.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
This paper demonstrates a practical, backbone-agnostic way to insert a learned DNN denoiser as the regularisation/deconvolution step inside classical optimisation loops that already contain the visibility→gridding→(i)FFT data-fidelity operator Φ, and shows this learned prior is highly robust to the training-data domain and to training-run randomness (quantifiable via ensembling) — directly relevant evidence that a similarly "plug-and-play" DNN deconvolution module could be swapped into the gridding/iFFT pipeline the UROP project targets, using SNR/logSNR/runtime as the metrics to benchmark such a substitution against CLEAN and optimisation-based baselines.
