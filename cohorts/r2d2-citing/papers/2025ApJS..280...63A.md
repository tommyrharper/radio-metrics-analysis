# Toward a Robust R2D2 Paradigm for Radio-interferometric Imaging: Revisiting Deep Neural Network Training and Architecture

**Bibcode:** 2025ApJS..280...63A
**Authors:** Amir Aghabiglou, Chung San Chu, Chao Tang, Arwa Dabbech, Yves Wiaux
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJS..280...63A/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02554 (arXiv:2503.02554v2 [astro-ph.IM], 1 Oct 2025)

## One-line summary
This paper upgrades the R2D2 deep-learning imaging paradigm for radio interferometry (VLA, monochromatic 512×512 intensity imaging) with a generalized/randomized training setup, a data-fidelity-based series convergence criterion, and a new U-WDSR DNN architecture (U-Net + WDSR residual blocks), showing consistent gains in reconstruction quality, data fidelity, computational efficiency, and epistemic uncertainty over the earlier R2D2 model, AIRI, uSARA, and multiscale CLEAN.

## Method
R2D2 forms an image as a series of residual images, each produced by a DNN that takes the current image estimate and its back-projected data residual (dirty residual image) as input, with a non-negativity projection applied at each step (a "learned CLEAN" where minor cycles are replaced by DNNs). This paper's contributions: (1) a generalized training set (T2) that randomizes integration time, uses multiscan/multinoise configurations, and randomizes pixel resolution (super-resolution factor) and Briggs weighting robustness parameter, versus the earlier fixed setup (T1); (2) a convergence criterion that halts the DNN series once the residual dirty image is compatible with the noise level (or relative variation/ fluctuation criteria), rather than always using the full DNN series, which also enables pruning of solved training/validation samples to cut training cost; (3) a new core architecture, U-WDSR, combining U-Net's encoder-decoder/skip-connection structure with a 16-block WDSR residual body (wide activation, dense skip connections, weight normalization, low-rank convolutions). Models are labeled R2D2_{A,T} for architecture A∈{A1=U-Net, A2=U-WDSR} and training setup T∈{T1,T2}. Benchmarks are run against uSARA, AIRI (both re-implemented as GPU-accelerated Python in BASPLib), and multiscale CLEAN (WSClean), on simulated VLA data (200–1000 inverse problems from real radio images: 3C 353, Messier 106, Abell 2034, PSZ2 G165.68+44.01) and on real Cygnus A VLA observations.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **SNR (signal-to-noise ratio, dB)** — linear-scale reconstruction fidelity metric: SNR(x̂,x⋆) = 20·log10(‖x⋆‖₂ / ‖x⋆ − x̂‖₂). Primary quantitative fidelity-to-ground-truth metric.
- **logSNR (dB)** — SNR computed after a per-image logarithmic intensity transform (parametrized by target dynamic range a: r_log(x) = x_max·log_a(a/x_max·x + 1)), giving a metric more sensitive to faint structure/low-intensity regions in high-dynamic-range images.
- **RDR (residual-to-dirty image ratio, ×10⁻³ typical scale)** — image-domain **data-fidelity** metric: RDR(r̂, x_d) = ‖r̂‖₂ / ‖x_d‖₂. Lower = higher data fidelity (residual closer to pure noise).
- **MRU (mean relative uncertainty)** — epistemic-uncertainty metric derived from pixel-wise relative uncertainty images [σ/µ](X_c) = (std/mean) across an ensemble of R reconstructions (either R different random-initialization realizations, or R different Briggs-weighting choices), restricted to pixels above 1/target-dynamic-range; MRU is the spatial average of this map.
- **Computational cost metrics**: number of series iterations I, total reconstruction time t_tot. (s), average per-iteration data-fidelity time t_dat., average per-iteration regularization/DNN time t_reg., all reported for 4 different measurement-operator (Φ†Φ) implementations (TorchKbNufft, PyNUFFT, FINUFFT, PSF-approximation). Also training cost in GPU-hours (Table 2).

**Reported quantitative results (mean ± std over 200 inverse problems unless noted):**
- Architecture/training ablation (Table 3): on the harder, fully-randomized test set E2, R2D2_A1,T1 (old U-Net + old training) collapses to SNR 20.2±12.0 dB / logSNR 12.4±12.2 dB, while R2D2_A2,T2 (new U-WDSR + new training) reaches SNR 31.2±2.4 dB / logSNR 24.6±4.2 dB — both much higher and far more stable (lower std).
- Benchmark comparison (Table 4, setup E2): CLEAN 12.0±19.3 dB SNR / 9.4±18.9 dB logSNR (and diverged on 3 of the test problems); uSARA 28.1±3.4 dB / 20.4±3.4 dB; AIRI 28.3±3.1 dB / 21.1±3.8 dB; standalone end-to-end U-Net and U-WDSR (1 iteration only) perform poorly (17.9±3.0 / 6.8±3.9 dB and 16.0±3.6 / 6.6±3.8 dB); R2D2_A1,T2 30.0±3.0 dB / 23.4±4.2 dB; **R2D2_A2,T2 (best model) 31.2±2.4 dB / 24.6±4.2 dB** — R2D2 models outperform uSARA/AIRI by roughly 2–4 dB in both SNR and logSNR.
- Data fidelity (RDR, Table 4): R2D2_A2,T2 2.22×10⁻³, AIRI 2.24×10⁻³, uSARA 2.15×10⁻³ (comparable, best group); CLEAN ~50% higher (3.29×10⁻³); R2D2_A1,T2 about twice as high (4.07×10⁻³) as R2D2_A2,T2.
- Compute/speed (Table 4): R2D2_A1,T2 needs 18.3±5.6 iterations, R2D2_A2,T2 needs 15.8±5.5 iterations, vs. AIRI fixed at 5000 iterations and uSARA ~1482 iterations; total reconstruction time for R2D2 is a few seconds (e.g., R2D2_A2,T2 ~5.6–9.0 s depending on NUFFT backend) versus several minutes for AIRI/uSARA — R2D2 offers a large speed advantage while matching or beating their fidelity/data-fidelity metrics.
- Epistemic uncertainty (Section 4.5–4.6, Fig. 3–5): across R2D2 realizations, R2D2_A2,T2 reaches ~2.5× lower steady-state MRU standard deviation than R2D2_A1,T2; across Briggs-weighting (ρ_br) variation, R2D2_A2,T2 achieves ~8× lower MRU standard deviation at convergence than R2D2_A1,T2. Best SNR at ρ_br=0 for both models; R2D2_A2,T2 peaks in logSNR at ρ_br=1 (natural weighting), consistent with theoretical expectation of higher dynamic range under natural weighting.
- Real data (Cygnus A, Section 5, Fig. 6): iteration counts to convergence were 12 (R2D2_A1,T1), 16 (R2D2_A1,T2), 15 (R2D2_A2,T2). RDR values: R2D2_A1,T1 2.85×10⁻³, R2D2_A1,T2 2.68×10⁻³, R2D2_A2,T2 2.46×10⁻³ (best/lowest, i.e., highest data fidelity). MRU (mean image over R=25 realizations/weightings): R2D2_A1,T2 8.98×10⁻², R2D2_A2,T2 2.37×10⁻² — roughly 4× lower uncertainty for the U-WDSR model. The U-WDSR model recovered all 3 highlighted faint features near the Cygnus A core/jets consistently, versus only 1–2 for the U-Net models, and produced more homogeneous (less structured) residuals around calibration-error-affected hotspots.
- Training compute (Table 2): total GPU-hours for full R2D2 series training: R2D2_A1,T2 231.6 GPU·hr (25 iterations, 31M-parameter U-Net), R2D2_A2,T2 420.9 GPU·hr (25 iterations, 20.9M-parameter U-WDSR); standalone single-network training costs 85.6 GPU·hr (U-Net) and 165.7 GPU·hr (U-WDSR).

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
This paper is directly on-topic: R2D2 replaces CLEAN's iterative minor-cycle deconvolution with a learned DNN series operating on the dirty image / back-projected data residual (i.e., the gridded-and-iFFT'd visibility data), making it a concrete template for where and how to insert ML into the gridding→iFFT→deconvolution stage. Its evaluation protocol (SNR/logSNR for fidelity to ground truth, RDR for data/residual fidelity, MRU for epistemic uncertainty, plus iteration counts and wall-clock/GPU-hour cost against CLEAN/AIRI/uSARA) offers a ready-made, quantitative benchmark suite for evaluating any ML-based replacement or augmentation of the deconvolution step in the pipeline being developed for this UROP project.
