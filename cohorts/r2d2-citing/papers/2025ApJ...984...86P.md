# Theoretical Foundation of Black Hole Image Reconstruction Using PRIMO

**Bibcode:** 2025ApJ...984...86P
**Authors:** Dimitrios Psaltis, Feryal Özel, Lia Medeiros, Tod R. Lauer
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...984...86P/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10322 (arXiv:2408.10322 [astro-ph.IM])

## One-line summary
This is a theoretical/analytic (not benchmark-driven) paper that justifies why PRIMO — the PCA/eigenimage dictionary-learning algorithm previously used to reconstruct the M87* EHT image — can faithfully reconstruct sparse-interferometry black-hole images without generating information unconstrained by the data, by showing that the Fourier-plane correlation length of any compact source is set by (and comparable to) the baseline length that resolves that source.

## Method
The paper does not present new observations or a new reconstruction; instead it derives, analytically and via a numerical experiment on 100 GRMHD-simulated black-hole snapshots, the relationship between an image's finite angular extent and the correlation length of its visibility function in the Fourier (u,v) plane. Using the van Cittert–Zernike theorem, a windowing/autocorrelation argument (Eq. 3–5), and a locally-defined "characteristic scale" us, vs (Eq. 6–12) computed from first/second visibility derivatives, they show the Fourier correlation scale equals the baseline u0 that resolves the image, essentially independent of image structure (confirmed numerically: distribution of us/u0, vs/v0 sharply peaked at unity, Fig. 4). They then show that for the 2017 EHT M87 array (5 sites, gaps ≳2 Gλ, resolving baseline u0≈2 Gλ), this correlation length gives near-complete "azimuthal coverage" of the Fourier plane out to ~8 Gλ (Fig. 5), explaining why only Ni≈20 PCA eigenimages (out of the PRIMO dictionary learned from GRMHD training images) suffice to reconstruct the image to ≲2% Fourier-plane residual. They also test eigenimage completeness/generality (azimuthal and radial functional forms resembling e^{imφ} and node-dense radial functions, Fig. 6) to argue PRIMO is not simply reproducing training-set biases.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **Fourier-plane correlation/resolving-baseline framework (not a conventional image metric):** derives that the correlation length of the visibility autocorrelation function A(δu,δv) for any compact image of angular size θ_im equals ~√2·u0, where u0 is the baseline that "resolves" the image (visibility amplitude drops to half its central value) — i.e., resolution and Fourier-coherence scale are theoretically tied together (Eq. 3–5, 11).
- **Characteristic Fourier-space variation scale us, vs (Eq. 6–8, 12):** locally defined via first/second numerical derivatives of the visibility function; used as a per-image, per-orientation resolution/coherence diagnostic, evaluated on 100 simulated GRMHD snapshots (5 spins × multiple field topologies) out to baseline lengths ≤20 Gλ. Distribution of us/u0, vs/v0 is narrowly peaked near 1 (Fig. 4) — the key quantitative validation of the theory.
- **Fractional azimuthal Fourier-plane coverage (Fig. 5):** fraction of a given baseline-length circle within one correlation length (2 Gλ) of an actual EHT measurement; ~perfect coverage up to ~8 Gλ for the 2017 M87 array, used as the operational justification for PRIMO's reliability at that resolution.
- **PCA/eigenimage compactness:** Ni≃20 principal components needed so that projection residual of the training-set images is ≲ the ~1% EHT data error (citing Medeiros et al. 2023a: <2% Fourier-plane residual, >99% variance explained with 20 components); related to an estimated ~16 independent "resolution elements" in the Fourier plane, (π·8²)/(π·2²)=16.
- **Super-resolution / theoretical resolution limit (Eq. 14, key result):** derives θ_min ≳ (1/θ_res + 1/θ_im)^{-1}, where θ_res~1/b_max is the array's nominal (diffraction-limited) resolution and θ_im~1/b0 is the source's own angular size — i.e. no algorithm (ML or otherwise) can recover structure finer than this combined limit without injecting unconstrained information; this generalizes and bounds "super-resolution" claims made for CNN- and GAN-based EHT imaging (citing Schmidt et al. 2022; Connor et al. 2022; Geyer et al. 2023) as well as regularized-maximum-likelihood super-resolution (Honma et al. 2014).
- **Regularizer derived from the theory (Eq. 13):** proposes a physically-motivated regularization term S = α[(us/u0)² + (vs/v0)²] for use in regularized maximum-likelihood (RML) reconstructions (cf. Chael et al. 2016), noting compact images are always characterized by S≲1 — offered as a non-ad-hoc alternative to brightness-gradient penalties (Candès et al. 2005), and explicitly noting PRIMO itself needs no such regularizer.
- **Comparison to CLEAN / RML:** notes the original M87 (2019) and Sgr A* (2022) EHT images were reconstructed primarily with RML methods and CLEAN-based approaches (EHTC 2019c, 2022b); PRIMO (Medeiros et al. 2023b) is described as producing Fourier components "statistically consistent with the observed data without requiring ad hoc regularizers" — used here as the qualitative fidelity benchmark motivating the present theoretical justification, rather than a new quantitative comparison.
- **Eigenimage completeness diagnostic (Fig. 6):** qualitative check that low-order PRIMO eigenimages' azimuthal (~e^{imφ}) and radial (dense minima/node coverage) functional forms span a sufficiently general/complete basis, supporting the claim that reconstructions depend only marginally on the specific GRMHD training set used.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
Provides a rigorous, generalizable theoretical framework — resolving-baseline-to-Fourier-correlation-length correspondence, and the derived resolution/super-resolution bound (Eq. 14) — for reasoning about when an ML-based deconvolution/gridding-gap-filling step (PCA-based, CNN, GAN, or diffusion-like) can validly infer missing Fourier information versus when it is fabricating unconstrained structure; also yields a concrete, physically motivated Fourier-domain regularizer usable in RML-style pipelines.
