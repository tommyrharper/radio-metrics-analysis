# Learned iterative networks: An operator learning perspective

**Bibcode:** 2025arXiv251208444H
**Authors:** Andreas Hauptmann (Research Unit of Mathematical Sciences, University of Oulu, Finland; Dept. of Computer Science, University College London, UK), Ozan Öktem (Dept. of Mathematics, KTH Royal Institute of Technology, Sweden)
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251208444H/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08444

## One-line summary
A review/book-chapter presenting a unified operator-theoretic framework for learned iterative reconstruction networks (unrolled optimisation, plug-and-play, deep equilibrium, learned primal-dual), separating "how to compute" (the reconstruction operator) from "what to compute" (the learning problem).

## Method
The paper formalises learned iterative networks — formed by unrolling classical iterative optimisation algorithms for variational inverse problems — in a functional-analytic operator-learning setting rather than the usual purely discrete view. It defines a learned reconstruction operator R: Y→X and treats its construction (architecture) separately from the learning problem that fits it (loss/data regime). Within this framework it classifies methods by update-direction scheme: learned gradient networks (learned least-squares and learned proximal networks, including plug-and-play/PnP and deep equilibrium/DEQ variants as special or limiting cases), learned primal-dual architectures, and higher-order/nonlinear extensions. It also distinguishes learning-problem setups: supervised (paired data), and unsupervised variants (learning-to-optimize, self-supervised, ground-truth-prior sampling, unpaired data). Both linear and nonlinear inverse problems are covered, situating this family (which includes R2D2/AIRI-style learned-iterative methods) as closely related variations on a common operator template.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- No PSNR/SSIM or other standard image-quality numbers were found in the accessible text; the paper's "short numerical study" (Section 10, on choice of update direction) exists but its concrete results were not retrievable from the available extraction.
- Evaluation emphasis is qualitative/structural rather than benchmark-driven: numerical stability, generalisation across noise level/instrumentation/acquisition protocol variation, and parameter efficiency (e.g., ~10^5 params in 2D / ~10^8 in 3D vs. much larger fully data-driven baselines such as AUTOMAP) are cited as evidence of good behaviour.
- Theoretical guarantees discussed in lieu of empirical metrics:
  - Convergence to a fixed point for plug-and-play/DEQ-type schemes, contingent on the composed update operator being **contractive** (constraining the Lipschitz constant of the learned network jointly with step-size choice; citing Chan 2016, Ryu 2019).
  - Explicit acknowledgment that most structured data-driven approaches still **lack formal guarantees** — no general sample complexity results, accuracy guarantees, or generalisation-gap estimates.
  - Noted trade-off: architectural restrictions that buy provable convergence/stability typically reduce network expressivity and **degrade quantitative performance**.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
Provides the theoretical/taxonomic backbone for R2D2- and AIRI-style learned-iterative deconvolution: it frames such networks as operator-learning instances of learned gradient/primal-dual schemes, and flags convergence/stability guarantees (via contractiveness and Lipschitz control) as the key open theoretical lever, rather than benchmark metrics, for justifying ML-based deconvolution replacements.
