# Scalable Bayesian uncertainty quantification with data-driven priors for radio interferometric imaging

**Bibcode:** 2024RASTI...3..505L
**Authors:** Tobías I. Liaudat, Matthijs Mars, Matthew A. Price, Marcelo Pereyra, Marta M. Betcke, Jason D. McEwen
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024RASTI...3..505L/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00125 (arXiv:2312.00125v2; also academic.oup.com/rasti/article/3/1/505/7727811, DOI 10.1093/rasti/rzae030)

## One-line summary
QuantifAI is a Bayesian radio-interferometric imaging method that pairs a physically motivated likelihood with a convex, data-driven (learned CRR-NN) prior to guarantee a log-concave posterior, enabling fast MAP-based image reconstruction plus scalable, MCMC-free uncertainty quantification (hypothesis testing, local credible intervals, and a novel fast pixel-wise UQ-at-scale method).

## Method
The posterior is built from a Gaussian-noise RI likelihood (visibility measurement operator Φ, i.e. Fourier sampling/gridding) combined with a Convex Ridge Regularizer Neural Network (CRR-NN) prior — a DNN-based regulariser trained on natural images but constrained to be convex, guaranteeing the full posterior is log-concave. The MAP estimate is computed via FISTA (accelerated proximal gradient). Because the posterior is log-concave and high-dimensional, the authors exploit probability-concentration results (Pereyra 2017) to approximate Highest Posterior Density (HPD) credible regions directly from the MAP potential, without needing MCMC sampling. This underpins three UQ tools: (1) hypothesis testing of image structure (is a given feature physical, e.g. via an inpainted or blurred "surrogate" image test), (2) Local Credible Intervals (LCIs) — pixel/superpixel-wise Bayesian error bars computed via root-finding on the HPD boundary, and (3) a novel "fast pixel UQ" method that computes error bars at multiple wavelet scales using a single nonlinear equation solve instead of repeated LCI evaluations. Experiments use four astronomical test images (M31, W28, Cygnus A, 3C288) with simulated Gaussian-noise visibilities (ISNR = 30 dB, 10% Fourier-plane coverage, gridded FFT setting), compared against a wavelet-based sparsity-promoting prior (à la Cai et al. 2018b / SARA-style) and validated against Langevin-based MCMC (SK-ROCK) sampling. A second experiment (Section 6) uses realistic ungridded MeerKAT visibility patterns (NUFFT forward model) at four synthesis times (1, 2, 4, 8 h).

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **Reconstruction SNR (dB):** SNR(x, x_gt) = −20·log₁₀(‖x_gt − x‖₂/‖x_gt‖₂), the primary image-fidelity metric vs. ground truth. Table 1 reports Dirty / wavelet-based(MMSE, MAP) / QuantifAI(MMSE, MAP) SNR for all four images: W28 (3.39, 18.17, 23.04, 23.38, **26.85**), M31 (5.01, 23.78, 25.52, 24.61, **27.48**), 3C288 (7.02, 14.31, 14.15, 23.23, **24.10**), Cygnus A (4.60, 20.52, 17.53, 25.36, **30.25**). QuantifAI's MAP reconstruction beats the wavelet-based MAP by 1.9–12.7 dB (average ~7 dB gain) across the dataset, and MAP consistently beats MMSE for both models.
- **Highest Posterior Density (HPD) credible regions:** defined at credible level 100(1−α)% (Eq. 29–32); approximated analytically from the MAP potential via a concentration bound (Pereyra 2017), avoiding MCMC. Used as the basis for all downstream UQ tools.
- **Hypothesis testing of image structure:** a region-of-interest is inpainted (Table 2) or Gaussian-blurred (σ=3.5 px, Table 3) to build a "surrogate" image, and its potential (f+g) is compared to the 99% HPD isocontour γ̂₀.₀₁ (α=0.01). Table 2 (inpainting) and Table 3 (blurring) report the initial/surrogate potentials and isocontour thresholds for MAP vs. SK-ROCK (MCMC) estimates on all four images; MAP-based and MCMC-based hypothesis-test outcomes agree in every case reported. QuantifAI correctly rejects "artefact" hypotheses for physical structures in M31, W28 and 3C288 (both regions), but is inconclusive for a small physical feature in Cygnus A.
- **Local Credible Intervals (LCIs):** per-superpixel Bayesian error bars, l_i = ξ+,Ωi − ξ−,Ωi, computed by root-finding the HPD boundary (Eq. 36–39); validated qualitatively against posterior-sample-based standard deviation. Mean LCI values reported for 16×16 superpixels: 0.20, 0.08, 0.24, 0.07 (image order as in their Figure 7); higher mean LCI = higher uncertainty (M31 and 3C288 flagged as more uncertain).
- **Fast pixel UQ at multiple scales:** validated by comparing predicted error to the true MAP-vs-ground-truth error decomposed at wavelet multi-resolution levels (Eq. 53); found good agreement (with some conservative overestimation), reproducing the correct qualitative error pattern per scale.
- **Compute cost / scalability metrics** (Table 4, W28 image, Nvidia A100 GPU, wall-clock seconds): MAP optimisation — wavelet-based 0.94 s vs. QuantifAI 0.64 s; Posterior (MCMC) sampling — wavelet-based 36.0×10³ s vs. QuantifAI 6.44×10³ s; LCIs (8×8 superpixels) — wavelet-based 149.7 s vs. QuantifAI 108.2 s; Fast pixel UQ — QuantifAI only, 0.17 s. MCMC sampling is ~60× slower than 8×8 LCIs and >37,500× slower than the fast pixel UQ; the fast pixel UQ is >630× faster than 8×8 LCIs.
- **Number of measurement-operator evaluations** (Table 5, W28): MCMC sampling 11×10⁶; LCIs (8×8) 81.5×10³; LCIs (16×16) 21.2×10³; Fast pixel UQ 28. The fast pixel UQ uses 3 and 6 orders of magnitude fewer likelihood/operator evaluations than LCIs and MCMC sampling respectively (headline claim also stated in the abstract).
- **Realistic ungridded-visibility experiment (MeerKAT, Table 6, M31 image):** as synthesis time increases (1/2/4/8 h; 3×10⁴–2.4×10⁵ visibilities), MAP reconstruction SNR rises 25.29 → 28.39 → 31.94 → 34.42 dB, with reconstruction wall-clock time 17.0–105.3 s and UQ (fast pixel) wall-clock time only 0.28–1.26 s — full UQ-enabled reconstruction achievable in well under two minutes even at ~2.4×10⁵ visibilities. Equivalent tables for W28, Cygnus A and 3C288 given in the appendix.
- **Baselines compared:** dirty image (naturally weighted), a wavelet-based sparsity-promoting MAP/MMSE prior (Cai et al. 2018b style, i.e. a SARA-like proximal-optimisation baseline), and Langevin MCMC (SK-ROCK) sampling as the UQ "ground truth" for validation. CLEAN is discussed only qualitatively in the introduction/related work, not run as a numerical baseline. The paper's related-work section explicitly discusses Aghabiglou et al. (2023) — an early version of the R2D2 paradigm — and AIRI (Terris et al. 2022), noting these DNN-based approaches "achieved similar or better performance than competing prior-based approaches whilst providing a significant acceleration," but does not benchmark QuantifAI numerically against R2D2/AIRI directly (no shared-dataset SNR comparison table).

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
QuantifAI shows how a learned, convex deconvolution prior (CRR-NN) can be dropped into a physically-motivated likelihood/measurement-operator pipeline (gridding/NUFFT-based Φ) to get both a competitive point estimate (MAP, 2–13 dB SNR gain over a classical sparsity prior) and calibrated, MCMC-free uncertainty maps at negligible extra compute (fast pixel UQ ~0.17–1.3 s) — a directly transferable template for adding well-calibrated uncertainty quantification to an ML-based deconvolution stage (e.g. R2D2/AIRI-style networks) in the visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution pipeline, using SNR, HPD-based credible intervals, and likelihood-evaluation counts as the metrics to benchmark such an addition.
