# S-R2D2: a spherical extension of the R2D2 deep neural network series paradigm for wide-field radio-interferometric imaging

**Bibcode:** 2025MNRAS.542..426T
**Authors:** A. Tajja, A. Aghabiglou, E. Tolley, J-P. Kneib, J-P. Thiran, Y. Wiaux
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.542..426T/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01462 (arXiv:2503.01462v1 [astro-ph.IM])

## One-line summary
S-R2D2 extends the planar R2D2 "Residual-to-Residual DNN series" imaging paradigm to wide-field, spherical radio-interferometric imaging by wrapping a fast Fourier-based sphere-to-plane interpolator (and its adjoint) around R2D2's 2D-Euclidean U-Net iterative scheme, letting the DNNs jointly learn to reconstruct the sky and correct interpolation approximations.

## Method
The wide-field RI measurement model maps a spherical intensity image to visibilities via the composition of a sphere-to-plane interpolator Γ (implemented efficiently as FFT + nonuniform FFT, i.e. type-1/type-2 NUFFT) and the standard planar RI operator Φp. Because gridding the sphere at the resolution required by the classical McEwen & Wiaux (2011) resolution rule is computationally impractical for wide fields of view, the authors deliberately operate Γ at a lower-than-optimal planar resolution (parameterised by a super-resolution factor SR) and instead train the R2D2-style iterative DNN series to correct the resulting interpolation errors. The training loss and residual/estimate updates are computed on the plane but consistency with the spherical ground truth is enforced by applying the adjoint interpolator Γ† before the non-negativity projection at every iteration (Algorithms 3–4), so the same 2D-Euclidean U-Net architecture used in R2D2 can be reused without redesigning spherical CNNs (which they found too slow/inaccurate/hard to tune in preliminary tests, citing Perraudin et al. 2019). Training/validation/test datasets of high-dynamic-range spherical ground truths (10,000 / 250 / 60 images respectively, DR ∈ [10^3, 5×10^5]) were synthetically generated from real astronomical images (NRAO, LOFAR/LoTSS-DR2, HBA Virgo cluster survey) and medical MRI images, back-projected onto the sphere via ray-tracing, exponentiated for high dynamic range, and radially tapered; VLA-type sampling patterns were simulated with MeqTrees/WSClean. S-R2D2 is compared head-to-head against planar R2D2 (adapted post-hoc with a final Γ† + non-negativity step for fair spherical comparison) across three planar resolutions Np = 400², 600², 800² (SR = 1.5, 2.25, 3).

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **SNR (signal-to-noise ratio, dB):** SNR(x*,x̂) = 20·log10(‖x*‖2 / ‖x*−x̂‖2), computed on the sphere between ground truth and reconstruction. Standard image-domain fidelity metric (same as used in Aghabiglou et al. 2024, 2025).
- **logSNR (dB):** SNR computed after applying a logarithmic pixel-wise transform rlog(x,a) = x_max·log_a(a·x/x_max + 1), with the parameter a fixed to the target's dynamic range (DR). This emphasises faint/low-intensity structures and dynamic-range recovery, which linear SNR under-weights.
- **RDR — residual-to-dirty image ratio:** RDR(r,x^d) = ‖r‖2/‖x^d‖2, a data-fidelity metric computed on the sphere (residual and dirty image back-projected via Γ†); measures how well the reconstruction's visibility-domain residual has been driven down relative to the initial dirty image, i.e. consistency with the measured visibility data (0 = drives noise to zero, i.e. perfect fit).
- **Runtime / compute cost (GPU hours / seconds on an Nvidia A40 48GB):** total reconstruction time t_tot = I×(t_reg + t_dat), split into regularisation-step time (t_reg, DNN forward pass + Γ†) and data-fidelity-step time (t_dat, residual computation with Φp and Γ). Fixed number of iterations I = 10 (chosen because logSNR stopped improving beyond that on validation data).
- **Training-set pruning fraction** (dataset-size reduction curve across iterations) used as a secondary indicator of how efficiently each method "solves" the underlying inverse problems during training (not a reconstruction-quality metric per se).
- **Quantitative comparison table (Table 1, means ± std over 60 test problems, DR ∈ [10^3,5×10^5], final iteration I=10):**
  - R2D2: SNR 6.6±1.5 dB (Np=400²) → 1.9±1.5 dB (Np=800²) — performance *degrades* with resolution; logSNR 7.3±3.2 → 1.6±1.3 dB; RDR 0.8×10⁻² → 14×10⁻² (worsens); t_tot ≈ 2.1–2.5 s.
  - S-R2D2: SNR 21.7±4.3 dB (Np=400²), 21.2±3.9 dB (Np=600², best logSNR), 20.8±3.7 dB (Np=800²); logSNR 17.5±3.2 → 18.7±2.9 (peak at Np=600²) → 15.9±3.5 dB; RDR 1.5×10⁻² → 1.2×10⁻² (best) → 2.1×10⁻²; t_tot ≈ 3.0–3.3 s.
  - S-R2D2 outperforms R2D2 by roughly **14–19 dB in SNR** and **10–17 dB in logSNR** at matched resolutions, while being **less than 1.5× slower** (~3 s vs ~2–2.5 s total reconstruction time) — both remain orders of magnitude faster than classical/optimisation baselines.
  - Best overall operating point identified: SR = 2.25 (Np = 600²), balancing interpolation accuracy against super-resolution difficulty; S-R2D2 still performs "satisfactorily" at the lowest resolution Np_min = 400² (same pixel size on sphere and plane), which is more computationally favourable.
- **Comparison to non-DNN/other baselines (qualitative, inherited from prior R2D2 papers, not re-run here):** citing Aghabiglou et al. (2024, 2025), R2D2 in the small-field/planar regime was shown to be more than an order of magnitude faster than uSARA, AIRI (Terris et al. 2025), and CLEAN at comparable/superior precision; this paper does not re-benchmark S-R2D2 directly against CLEAN, uSARA, or AIRI on the sphere (left to future work), only against planar R2D2 itself as the baseline. SR enhancement limit of 3 was chosen as "a standard limit in high-DR frameworks for similar algorithms such as CLEAN and uSARA" (Terris et al. 2022).
- **Ablation/robustness view:** metrics are also tracked per-iteration (Fig. 8) and across four individual test images (galaxy clusters Abell 2034, PSZ2 G165.68+44.01, radio galaxies 3C354, Messier 106) at varying DR (8.9×10⁴ to 2.3×10⁵), each reporting per-image (SNR, logSNR) values and RDR at each Np, demonstrating robustness of S-R2D2's improvement across sources and dynamic-range regimes, whereas R2D2's data fidelity (RDR) degrades sharply as Np/SR increases (its "super-resolving" limitation).

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
This paper is directly relevant: it shows how to insert a differentiable, FFT-based gridding/interpolation step (sphere↔plane, analogous to the visibility gridding stage) directly into a learned iterative deconvolution series, training the network's loss and residual computation to jointly correct gridding/interpolation error rather than treating gridding as a fixed pre-processing step — a template for how ML deconvolution/imaging networks in the standard visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution pipeline could be made aware of and compensate for approximations introduced earlier in the pipeline (e.g., degridding kernels, w-projection, non-negligible interpolation error), while using standard 2D CNN architectures and metrics (SNR, logSNR, RDR) as the evaluation framework.
