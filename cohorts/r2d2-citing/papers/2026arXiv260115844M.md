# Radio-Interferometric Image Reconstruction with Denoising Diffusion Restoration Models

**Bibcode:** 2026arXiv260115844M
**Authors:** M. Morales, E. Tolley, R. Poitevineau (Institute of Physics, Laboratory of Astrophysics, EPFL)
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260115844M/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15844 (arXiv:2601.15844v2 [astro-ph.IM])

## One-line summary
The authors train a DDPM as a data-driven prior for radio-galaxy morphology on VLA FIRST survey images, then use Denoising Diffusion Restoration Models (DDRM) — an unsupervised posterior-sampling scheme — to reconstruct sky images directly from simulated VLA/EHT/ALMA visibilities, showing large fidelity gains over CLEAN-family methods without needing to be retrained for each array configuration.

## Method
A 40M-parameter U-Net DDPM (ResNet blocks + FiLM conditioning) is trained unconditionally for 300,000 optimizer steps on 21,758 flux-normalized 150×150-pixel VLA FIRST radio-galaxy images (Griese et al. 2023 + Radio Galaxy Zoo DR1), with 200/200 validation/test images held out. This DDPM serves purely as a learned prior p(x₀); it is not trained on any specific antenna layout. At inference, DDRM (Kawar et al. 2022) performs approximate posterior sampling p(x₀|y) by taking the SVD of the linear measurement operator H = SF (sampling matrix × Fourier transform) and blending the DDPM's denoising steps with the observed visibilities y = Hx + z, guided by hyperparameters η=0.85 and η_b=1.0. Because the forward model is expressed generically via the measurement operator's SVD rather than baked into training, the same trained DDPM is reused across three simulated telescope configurations (VLA, EHT, ALMA) without retraining. Reconstruction is benchmarked against CLEAN, multi-scale CLEAN, and IUWT compressed sensing (MORESANE-style), all implemented via the Radler/WSClean library, plus an out-of-domain test on radio galaxy 3c353 (larger dynamic range, from Terris et al. 2023) and synthetic test images (letter "E", random point-source grid).

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **MSE (Mean Squared Error):** pixel-wise squared error averaged over image and over multiple stochastic DDRM reconstructions, MSE = (1/NM)ΣΣ(x⁽ʲ⁾ − x̂ᵢ⁽ʲ⁾)². Primary metric used throughout for method comparisons.
- **PSNR (Peak SNR):** PSNR = 10·log₁₀(MAX/MSE), MAX=1 (flux-normalized images). Reported alongside MSE "for comparison to other methods."
- **SNR:** SNR = 10·log₁₀[(1/N)Σx⁽ʲ⁾ / MSE].
- **SRE (Standardised Reconstruction Error):** SRE = (1/NM)ΣΣ|x̂ᵢ⁽ʲ⁾ − x⁽ʲ⁾|/σ⁽ʲ⁾, where σ⁽ʲ⁾ is the per-pixel std across an ensemble of DDRM samples. Used to test whether the DDPM's sample-to-sample spread is a well-calibrated uncertainty proxy (well-calibrated should give SRE ≤ 1); they find DDRM is **overconfident** (SRE up to 8 where faint components are missed), consistent with known DDPM miscalibration (Pang et al. 2023).
- **Sampling steps K vs. quality/runtime (Table 1, VLA, batch size 128, single NVIDIA GH200):**
  - K=10: MSE 3.2×10⁻⁵, PSNR 45.0, SNR 36.8, SRE 1.39, t=0.44 s
  - K=50: MSE 1.0×10⁻⁵, PSNR 49.9, SNR 41.7, SRE 1.55, t=2.21 s
  - K=100: MSE 6.6×10⁻⁶, PSNR 51.8, SNR 43.7, SRE 1.40, t=4.41 s
  - K=500: MSE 1.0×10⁻⁶, PSNR 59.8, SNR 51.7, SRE 1.44, t=22.03 s
  - K=1000: MSE 5.2×10⁻⁷, PSNR 62.9, SNR 54.7, SRE 1.04, t=45.47 s (used for all subsequent results)
  - EHT (K=1000): MSE 6.8×10⁻⁷, PSNR 61.7, SNR 53.5, SRE 1.11
  - ALMA (K=1000): MSE 5.25×10⁻⁷, PSNR 62.8, SNR 54.6, SRE 1.14
  - Even K=10 (0.44 s) gives strong MSE (3.2×10⁻⁵), demonstrating DDRM's speed/quality tradeoff flexibility.
- **Comparison to CLEAN / MS CLEAN / IUWT CS (compressed sensing):** measured via average per-image MSE across 200 test images and noise levels σ_y=0, 0.05, 0.10. DDRM's MSE is **at least 2 orders of magnitude smaller** than CLEAN, MS CLEAN, and IUWT CS at all noise levels, including heavily noisy data (σ_y=0.1, 10% of max flux). Visually, DDRM reconstructions show no residual dirty-beam artifacts (unlike CLEAN variants) and are "by eye" comparable to IUWT CS.
- **MSE vs. true pixel flux:** DDRM outperforms CLEAN across the flux range and especially in bright regions; IUWT CS is better than DDRM at very low flux values (attributed to its explicit sparsity prior vs. DDRM's residual sampling noise).
- **Robustness to noise misspecification:** running DDRM with an incorrect noise assumption (σ_y=0 regardless of true noise) still yields quality comparable to CLEAN/IUWT CS at true noise up to σ_y=0.1.
- **Out-of-domain generalization (3c353, larger dynamic range than training set):** DDRM outperforms MS CLEAN and is comparable to IUWT CS despite the DDPM never having seen this morphology/dynamic-range regime during training.
- **No LPIPS, SSIM, dynamic-range ratio, or χ² goodness-of-fit metrics are used** in this paper, despite being common in the broader literature — the authors rely specifically on MSE/PSNR/SNR/SRE plus qualitative/visual residual-map comparisons.
- **Computational complexity:** analytically derived as O(K·N·log N) (K sampling steps, N pixels), dominated by the FFT2 data-consistency step.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
This paper is highly relevant: instead of gridding visibilities and applying a CNN-based deconvolution network trained per array layout (as in R2D2/AIRI-style approaches), it embeds the ungridded, physics-exact SVD of the sampling+Fourier operator directly into a diffusion posterior-sampling loop, so a single pretrained DDPM prior generalizes across telescopes (VLA/EHT/ALMA) without retraining — a genuinely different architectural strategy for the visibility→image step worth comparing against R2D2/AIRI on the same MSE/PSNR/SNR metrics.
