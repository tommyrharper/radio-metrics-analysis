# Accelerating the CLEAN algorithm of radio interferometry with convex optimization

**Bibcode:** 2026A&A...706A..77M
**Authors:** Hendrik Müller, Mingyu Hsieh, Sanjay Bhatnagar
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026A%26A...706A..77M/abstract
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17410

## One-line summary
By recasting the Cotton–Schwab CLEAN major loop as a Newton scheme, the authors adapt two classical convex-optimization accelerants — Nesterov momentum and conjugate-gradient (CG) orthogonalization — to CLEAN's major-loop residual update, achieving several-times-to-order-of-magnitude faster convergence and deeper residual reduction than standard CLEAN, without touching the minor loop (deconvolution) at all, and at lower cost than swapping in an advanced minor-loop algorithm.

## Method
This is a purely classical (non-ML) optimization paper, included here as a non-DNN speed/fidelity baseline. The major loop of Cotton–Schwab CLEAN (gridding/degridding the residual visibilities against the current sky model) is shown to be mathematically equivalent to a Newton/gradient-descent step on the data-fidelity term. The authors then apply two well-known acceleration techniques from convex optimization to this step: (1) **Momentum-CLEAN**, which adds a Nesterov-style momentum term to the major-loop update (implemented as a weighted sum of consecutive residual images, costing about as much as one extra minor-loop iteration, no extra gridding/FFT), and (2) **CG-CLEAN**, which builds a conjugate-gradient/Krylov-subspace search direction across major-loop iterations (B-conjugacy) so successive updates are orthogonalized rather than repeating similar directions. Both are tested standalone and combined with an advanced minor-loop algorithm (Asp-CLEAN), across CASA tutorial data, synthetic Cygnus A, an ENZO simulation, real narrowband VLA data (M31, Cygnus A, G055.7+3.4), VLBA data (3C 120), and multi-channel spectral-line imaging (100 channels).

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **χ² data-fidelity/residual metric:** χ²(V, Iₖ) = Σᵢ ‖V(uᵢ,vᵢ) − (ΦIₖ)(uᵢ,vᵢ)‖²/σᵢ², i.e. weighted visibility-domain residual norm; used to track convergence per major-loop iteration.
- **Major-loop iteration count** (primary speed metric, since gridding/FFT cost is dominated by major loop cycles): CG-CLEAN reaches the same accuracy/dynamic range as CLEAN in roughly a **fifth of the major-loop iterations** on synthetic Cygnus A, and **three to five times fewer major-loop iterations** on real narrowband data (M31, Cygnus A, G055.7+3.4); similar gains confirmed on VLBA 3C 120 data (extrapolation to VLBI regime) and on 100-channel spectral cubes (with one channel showing a narrower margin due to minor-loop stalling).
- **PSNR (linear):** PSNR = 20 log₁₀(max(I)/‖θ−I‖), I = ground truth, θ = reconstruction.
- **PSNR (logarithmic):** PSNR_log = 20 log₁₀(max(log I)/‖log θ − log I‖), emphasizing diffuse/faint emission fidelity (analogous in spirit to logSNR metrics used in R2D2-family papers). CG-CLEAN and Momentum-CLEAN score highest on both PSNR variants on synthetic Cygnus A and an ENZO simulation, with CG-CLEAN outperforming Momentum-CLEAN and standard CLEAN overall.
- **Dynamic range:** on real narrowband data, CG-CLEAN "continues to attain higher dynamic ranges in later iterations" than CLEAN; combining CG-CLEAN with Asp-CLEAN in the minor loop reaches the noise floor after just a few major-loop iterations.
- **Computational cost / complexity:** Momentum-CLEAN's overhead is just an image-sum with the numerical cost of one minor-loop iteration (no added gridding/FFT); gains come purely from needing fewer major loops. The authors describe the combined (CG + Asp-CLEAN) approach as reaching close to **an order-of-magnitude improvement in convergence speed and dynamic range**, at lower numerical cost than upgrading the minor loop (advanced deconvolution algorithm) alone.
- **Convergence-rate behavior:** Momentum-CLEAN is fastest in the very first few iterations; CG-CLEAN starts comparable to plain CLEAN but overtakes both Momentum-CLEAN and CLEAN once the Krylov subspace/orthogonal search directions build up ("orthogonalization becomes effective only after a few iterations"), giving superlinear-type convergence later on.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity (Momentum-CLEAN):** momentum parameter μ tested over [0,1]; μ≈0.7 optimal for the M31 dataset, μ≥0.9 causes oscillatory/overcleaning behavior, μ=0.5 chosen as a robust default across datasets.
- **Baseline comparisons:** all results are benchmarked directly against standard (unaccelerated) Cotton–Schwab CLEAN, and separately against replacing the minor loop with an advanced deconvolution algorithm (Asp-CLEAN); the paper reports the major-loop acceleration alone is "competitive" with minor-loop upgrades but cheaper, and that combining both gives the best overall performance (with diminishing/saturating returns when stacked).

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
This paper is a pure classical-optimization (non-ML) baseline showing that major-loop convergence speed and residual/dynamic-range fidelity in the visibility-degridding/residual-update stage can be substantially improved via convex-optimization techniques (momentum, CG) without any learned components — establishing an iteration-count/wall-clock/dynamic-range reference point (e.g. 3–5× fewer major cycles, near order-of-magnitude speedups) that an ML-based gridding-to-deconvolution pipeline should be benchmarked against, and hinting that similar Newton/CG-style acceleration could in principle be combined with or inform learned iterative (R2D2-style) update schemes.
