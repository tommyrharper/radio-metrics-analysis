# An optimized algorithm for multi-scale wideband deconvolution of radio astronomical images

**Bibcode:** 2017MNRAS.471..301O
**Authors:** A. R. Offringa, O. Smirnov
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.471..301O/abstract
**Full text:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.06786
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06786
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stx1547

## One-line summary
The paper introduces WSClean's optimized fixed-scale subminor-loop multi-scale CLEAN and joined-channel wideband deconvolution, demonstrating similar or lower residuals than CASA multi-scale CLEAN with much faster minor iterations, while also showing that the tested MORESANE and IUWT convex-optimization variants can become unstable on realistic imperfect data.

## Method
For joined-channel deconvolution, WSClean divides the observing band into output channels and makes a dirty image and PSF for each. The minor loop selects each component's position from the full-bandwidth integrated image, measures its strength in every output channel, optionally fits those measurements with a polynomial or other spectral function, and subtracts the corresponding channel PSFs. It keeps a separate model image per output channel. Cotton-Schwab major cycles remain available for correcting w-terms and other direction-dependent effects, and prediction can use fitted intrinsic spectral polynomials whose coefficients account for channel-integrated rather than merely channel-centre flux.

The optimized multi-scale CLEAN first convolves the residual with every candidate scale kernel and selects the scale whose peak is largest after applying a scale bias. It then holds that scale fixed for a subminor loop. A Clark-like optimization restricts peak searches and PSF subtraction to an active set of pixels until the selected scale's peak has fallen by the multi-scale loop gain, after which the full residual is updated and all scales are reconsidered. The implementation supports tapered-quadratic and Gaussian kernels, automatically derives a geometric series of scale sizes from the synthesized beam, and uses a scale-dependent subtraction gain. Its scale-bias function gives adjacent scales a constant preference ratio; the default bias is 0.6.

Automatic scale-dependent masking first cleans to a detection threshold while recording a separate component-position mask for each scale, then cleans more deeply only at positions already detected at that same scale. An optional sliding-window local RMS prevents bright calibration artefacts from setting inappropriate thresholds elsewhere in a low-frequency field. The paper also wraps the image-space MORESANE solver in WSClean Cotton-Schwab major cycles and tests a joined-channel IUWT method that selects structures using full-bandwidth sensitivity, solves with conjugate gradients, segments connected components, and fits each component separately in every channel.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **MWA Vela/Puppis A residual fidelity:** On a real 2 min MWA observation, default CASA multi-scale CLEAN gives 64 mJy/PSF residual RMS, while WSClean with scale-bias 0.60 gives 50 mJy/PSF. The text reports 47 mJy/PSF for WSClean with bias 0.35 after disabling the two largest scales, but Figure 4 labels that same result 62 mJy/PSF; the paper does not resolve this discrepancy. The authors caution that the difference is affected by the two programs' scale-bias functions and gridders, and that CASA's bias was not tuned. The IUWT and MORESANE variants give 63 and 75 mJy/PSF respectively, with visually sharper models but no deeper residual image.
- **Automatic masking:** On the same MWA field, unmasked cleaning diverges at about 2 sigma if pushed toward 0.3 sigma. Cleaning first to 3 sigma and then to 0.3 sigma with the automatically generated per-scale mask converges and reduces residual RMS from 50 to 38 mJy/PSF, close to the 36 mJy/PSF system-noise estimate from Stokes V. On the simulated UGC12591 field, the masked WSClean residual is 8.6 arbitrary units/PSF and is visually noise-like; without masking, Figure 6 reports residual standard deviations of 37, 15, and 19 units/PSF for CASA multi-scale, WSClean multi-scale, and WSClean IUWT respectively.
- **Robustness on imperfect LOFAR data:** At 150 MHz, with 1.5 arcsec pixels and a 10 arcsec synthesized beam, WSClean multi-frequency multi-scale CLEAN reaches 1.4 mJy/PSF RMS and remains stable around a 1 Jy apparent source affected by ionospheric and primary-beam errors. IUWT reaches 2.7 mJy/PSF but diverges around that source and does not clean fainter sources adequately; MORESANE shows the same qualitative failure. This is a robustness demonstration rather than a controlled equal-quality benchmark.
- **Wideband simulation:** The test uses a 2 min zenith MWA synthesis over 30 MHz centred at 149 MHz. It contains two overlapping 2 degree FWHM Gaussians 1.5 degrees apart with spectral indices -1 and +1, 400 Jy integrated flux density and 35 mJy peak flux density, plus a 1 Jy point source with spectral index -2. All methods stop when the residual peak falls below three residual standard deviations. Figure 10 gives off-source residual RMS values of 880, 310, 2000, 460, and 63 microJy/PSF for single-scale CLEAN, multi-scale CLEAN, MORESANE, multi-frequency single-scale CLEAN, and multi-frequency multi-scale CLEAN respectively. The narrative rounds the last value to 64 microJy/PSF and calls it a factor of 4400 below the 280 mJy/PSF dirty-image RMS. Adding automatic scale-dependent masking reaches 55 microJy/PSF, reported as a factor of 5100 reduction. The multi-scale spectral-index map follows the input gradient but has local errors of a few tenths; the paper presents this qualitatively and does not report a spectral-index RMSE.
- **Real wideband Cygnus A test:** The JVLA subset spans 832 MHz centred at 3.47 GHz, contains about 25 h across A, B, and C configurations, and is imaged at 6 arcsec resolution on a 6120 x 6120 grid. The hotspot models are removed in visibility space after self-calibration and direction-dependent calibration. Multi-frequency IUWT does not converge. MORESANE, multi-scale CLEAN, and masked multi-scale CLEAN have visually similar off-source artefact levels but different residual morphology; masked multi-scale CLEAN is judged best for filamentary residual structure. No scalar RMS or dynamic-range value is reported for this comparison.
- **Single-frequency minor-loop speed:** Tests deconvolve a 2048 x 2048 image with six scales on a 40-core Intel Xeon E5-2660 v2 at 2.20 GHz with 128 GB RAM. They exclude inversion, prediction, scale-kernel initialization, and visibility gridding. CASA takes 348 s for 10,000 iterations, or 29 iterations/s, and an extrapolated 3480 s for 100,000. WSClean's three major cycles perform 142, 2409, and 97,449 minor iterations at averages of 5, 44, and 748 iterations/s, giving 223 s for 100,000 iterations, about 15.6 times faster than the CASA extrapolation.
- **Multi-frequency minor-loop speed:** With two Taylor terms and otherwise equivalent settings, CASA MSMFS runs at 0.42 iterations/s, implying 66 h for 100,000 iterations. WSClean joined-channel multi-scale CLEAN takes 265, 349, and 600 s for 100,000 iterations with 2, 4, and 8 output channels, respectively; its final major cycle reaches 771, 498, and 323 iterations/s. The comparison is therefore roughly 400-900 times faster, but covers only the minor loop, not an equal-fidelity end-to-end imaging run. Repeating on a four-core laptop makes both implementations about 2-3 times slower, supporting the claim that most of the relative gain is algorithmic rather than simply better parallelization.
- **Convex-optimization runtime context:** On the same 2048 x 2048 data, CPU MORESANE takes 256 min for single-frequency deconvolution, while IUWT takes 44 min for one channel and 179 min for eight channels. The corresponding WSClean multi-scale times are 223 s for single-frequency and 600 s for eight-channel joined cleaning. The MORESANE comparison uses its CPU implementation; the paper notes that PyMORESANE also has a faster GPU path but does not benchmark it.
- **Metric scope:** Quantitative fidelity is evaluated mainly with residual RMS or standard deviation and convergence, alongside visual model and residual morphology. Residual/dirty RMS ratios (e.g. factor ≈4400 below dirty RMS) are off-source residual RMS scores, not R2D2-style normalized RDR (‖r̂‖₂/‖x_dirty‖₂). Runtime is measured as wall-clock time and minor iterations per second with explicit image size and hardware. The paper does not report PSNR, SSIM, learned-model accuracy, or a controlled end-to-end wall time that includes gridding, inversion, prediction, and deconvolution.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
This work supplies a strong classical deconvolution baseline after visibility gridding and inverse FFT. Its joined-channel method preserves channel-specific dirty images, PSFs, and model fluxes while using the integrated band for more reliable component localization, which is directly relevant to an ML pipeline that must exploit spectral context without erasing per-channel information. Cotton-Schwab prediction also keeps the deconvolution stage connected to visibility-domain correction of non-coplanar and direction-dependent effects.

The evaluation highlights requirements for a fair learned replacement: report residual fidelity and photometric or spectral accuracy in addition to speed; preserve stopping thresholds, scale bias, channel count, image size, calibration quality, and hardware context; and distinguish minor-loop throughput from end-to-end visibility-to-image cost. The failures of IUWT and MORESANE on ionospheric, calibration-imperfect, and spectrally complex cases are especially relevant warnings against validating learned or sparse methods only on small, idealized simulations.
