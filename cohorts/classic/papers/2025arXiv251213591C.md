## One-line summary
astroCAMP defines a cross-layer benchmark and multi-objective co-design framework for comparing SKA-scale radio-imaging configurations by end-to-end performance, hardware behaviour, energy, carbon, cost, and scientific fidelity, and demonstrates a subset of those metrics on WSClean+IDG CPU/GPU runs and a model-based CPU/FPGA design-space exploration.

## Method
The framework separates benchmarking into three stages. First, it executes a fixed imaging workload under controlled conditions and records workflow timing, rack or node power, device telemetry, I/O, and resource counters. Second, it normalises those measurements into a common metric vector. Third, it explores algorithmic parameters, hardware choices, and workflow mappings as a multi-objective design space and identifies non-dominated configurations. A configuration is represented as `x = (a, h, s)`, where `a` covers choices such as precision, convolution-kernel size, w-stacking or NUFFT order, tiling, and task fusion; `h` covers processor and accelerator choices, memory hierarchy, power caps, and thread or block geometry; and `s` covers decomposition, data locality, I/O staging, buffering, batching, and orchestration.

The core optimisation minimizes time-to-solution, energy-to-solution, carbon-to-solution, and scalarised quality loss. Quality loss is `DeltaQ(x) = d(Q_ref, Q(x))`, a weighted distance from a high-fidelity reference in a quality space such as dirty-image RMS and PSNR. Feasible designs must remain within an allowed quality loss, an average-power cap, a minimum processing-throughput or survey-cadence requirement, and hardware resource bounds. Total cost of ownership and cost-to-solution are also part of the framework's metric backbone and objective space. The current implementation uses PREESM for system and platform design-space evaluation and CEO-DC for sustainability and economic calculations.

The released dataset uses OSKAR version `2.11.2-dev 2025-07-17 ddb65ed` and the full 512-station SKA-Low configuration. The paper enumerates 1, 8, 64, 128, and 256 timesteps and the same five channel counts, with 10 s per timestep, phase centre RA 25.0 degrees and Dec -30.0 degrees, a 151 MHz start frequency with 1 MHz channel increments, and GLEAM sources over a 40-degree field of view. It describes data volumes from megabytes to terabytes and supplies raw power and timing logs, energy metrics, a reference dirty image, and CPU/GPU configuration templates. The paper states that 16 datasets were produced, but also describes the five-by-five parameter grid and later says that 25 configurations at four image sizes produced 100 runs; these dataset counts are internally inconsistent.

The measured WSClean+IDG benchmark fixes the field of view and varies image size over 4096 x 4096, 8192 x 8192, 16,384 x 16,384, and 32,768 x 32,768 pixels, corresponding to 17.578, 8.789, 4.394, and 2.197 arcsec pixels. Each monitored run follows a CUDA-compiling warmup, a 120 s settling pause, the monitored execution, and another 120 s pause. Tests use exclusive access to a Lenovo ThinkSystem SR675 V3 KUMA node containing two AMD EPYC 9334 CPUs, 371 GB RAM, 6.4 TB NVMe, and four 94 GB NVIDIA H100 GPUs. Each run is allocated 16 CPU cores, one H100, and 92.2 GB RAM. Node PDU measurements use a 1 s power average sampled every 5 s and are cross-checked against CPU RAPL and GPU NVML through PMT. The economic model assumes a comparable server costs USD 29,875.39, embodies 513 kgCO2e including 176 kgCO2e for the H100, and amortises capital cost over six years.

The separate PREESM demonstration maps the inversion part of a DDFacet-like simulator across CPU-only and CPU-FPGA designs on a KRIA KR260 system-on-module. It uses a four-core 1.6 GHz ARM Cortex-A53 and a Xilinx Ultrascale+ FPGA at 150 MHz, maps a 128 x 128 two-dimensional FFT as two one-dimensional FFTs, and produces about 98,000 visibilities. It measures latency, resource occupancy, and energy above the board's idle baseline, then plots Pareto fronts for latency versus energy and latency versus occupancy.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **Metric roles and measurement types:** Table 2 contains 18 metric rows, with PSNR and SSIM sharing one row. Core metrics enter the optimisation; diagnostic metrics explain bottlenecks or improve reproducibility. Metrics are classified as directly measured, derived, or proxy/model-based. The list below preserves the table definitions and units. Not every defined metric is measured in the paper's experiments.
- **Time-to-solution:** `T_c`, seconds, core and direct, is full-system end-to-end elapsed wall-clock time from POSIX timing, workflow logs, or scheduler timestamps.
- **Energy-to-solution:** `E_c = integral_0^T_c P(t) dt`, joules, core and direct, integrates instantaneous system power over the workload using rack or node PDUs and CPU RAPL, GPU NVML, or PMT traces.
- **Throughput:** `Theta = N / T_c`, visibilities per second in the table, core and derived, divides the number of processed visibilities or images by elapsed runtime. The text also permits images per second.
- **Energy efficiency:** `eta_E = N / E_c`, visibilities per joule, core and derived, measures processed science data per unit of system energy.
- **Data locality:** `L_d = V_local / V_total`, dimensionless, diagnostic and proxy/model-based, is the fraction of I/O volume served locally, obtained from Darshan or filesystem telemetry.
- **Device utilisation:** `U = t_active / t_total`, dimensionless, diagnostic and direct, is the fraction of wall time during which a CPU, GPU, FPGA, or other device actively executes kernels.
- **Memory bandwidth:** `B_mem = Bytes / T_c`, GB/s, diagnostic and direct, measures sustained data movement and helps identify memory-bound execution.
- **Memory efficiency:** `eta_mem = Bytes / E_c`, GB/J, diagnostic and direct, divides transferred DRAM bytes by system energy-to-solution to expose the energy cost of memory traffic.
- **Peak memory:** `M_peak`, GB, diagnostic and direct, is the maximum resident CPU or accelerator memory footprint and constrains batch size and workload scaling.
- **Dirty-image RMS:** `sigma_dirty = sqrt((1/N) sum_i (I_i - mean(I))^2)`, Jy/beam, core and direct, measures dirty-image noise and artefact level from pixel intensities.
- **PSNR and SSIM:** PSNR is written in the paper as `10 log10(I_max / MSE)`, in dB, while SSIM is dimensionless; the paired row is core and direct. Both compare a reconstruction with a reference, with SSIM measuring structural similarity. The printed PSNR expression uses `I_max`, not `I_max^2`.
- **Dynamic range:** `DR = I_max / sigma_res`, dimensionless, core and direct, divides peak intensity by residual-image RMS to measure faint-emission detectability.
- **Astrometric error:** `epsilon_astro = (1/N) sum_i ||x_i - x_i_ref||`, arcsec or pixels, core and direct, averages catalogue position offsets between reconstructed and reference sources.
- **Carbon-to-solution:** `C_c = E_c kappa(t,r)`, gCO2e, core and proxy/model-based, combines measured job energy with time- and region-dependent grid carbon intensity.
- **Carbon efficiency:** `eta_C = N / C_c`, visibilities per gCO2e, diagnostic and derived, measures processed data per emitted carbon.
- **Total cost of ownership:** `C_TCO = C_capex + C_opex`, euros in the generic table, diagnostic and direct, combines lifetime capital and operational costs.
- **Cost per job:** `C_E = E_c p_E`, euros in the generic table, core and derived, multiplies job energy by the electricity tariff.
- **Cost efficiency:** `Theta / C_TCO`, visibilities per euro, diagnostic and derived, measures science operations delivered per lifetime system cost.
- **Additional fidelity measures are proposals, not benchmark results:** The surrounding text names photometric error and later calls for flux-scale accuracy, polarisation purity, rotation-measure recovery error, spectral-line fidelity, time-series accuracy, and transient completeness. These do not have formulas in Table 2 and are not measured in the released evaluation. The paper explicitly says algorithm-level quality metrics are excluded from the current release pending community agreement on programme-specific tolerances. No dirty-image RMS, PSNR, SSIM, dynamic range, astrometric error, photometric error, completeness, or restored-image fidelity result is reported for the WSClean+IDG runs.
- **Heterogeneous CPU/GPU energy and throughput findings:** At 32,768 x 32,768 pixels, GPU and IDG-device layers contribute about 60-90% of active-compute energy. GPU energy grows from about 5-10 kJ for small cases to about 165 kJ at 256 timesteps and 256 channels, while CPU and IDG-host energy stays nearly flat. Across the monitored workload grid, static energy, estimated from one quarter of PDU idle power times runtime, is about 80-85% of total energy; dynamic energy integrates GPU power plus one quarter of CPU power above idle. Throughput initially improves and then saturates while wall time and energy continue to grow, and channel-heavy cases provide similar throughput with less wall time and energy than time-heavy cases.
- **Memory and data-movement findings:** Output images occupy 0.0625, 0.25, 1.0, and 4.0 GiB at the four image sizes. Increasing channel count mainly grows input volume. GPU IDG generally has higher GB/J than the host path, but neither scales proportionally with payload size, so large configurations become input-dominated.
- **Roofline context:** The CPU reference ceilings are 922 GB/s DRAM bandwidth, 2,766 GFLOP/s FP64, and 5,532 GFLOP/s FP32; the H100 reference ceilings are 2.45 TB/s DRAM, 9.64 TB/s L2, and 27.12 TB/s L1. GPU gridder and sub-FFT kernels approach H100 L1/L2 regions only during active windows. CPU WSClean remains far below the FP64 ceiling, its throughput plateaus about one order of magnitude below the GPU path, and CPU utilisation rises only from about 2% at one thread to about 18% at 64 threads. The authors therefore attribute the end-to-end limit primarily to orchestration, synchronisation, and host-device supply rather than a single kernel roof.
- **CPU strong scaling and exact timing baseline:** For 256 timesteps, 256 channels, and a 16,384 x 16,384 image, CPU IDG wall times for 1, 16, 32, and 64 CPU cores are 3:13:10, 1:08:28, 1:08:10, and 1:08:06. GPU IDG wall times over the same CPU-core counts are 1:09:27, 1:09:57, 1:09:14, and 1:08:45. End-to-end CPU speedup saturates at 2.82x from 16 threads onward, only 17.6% of ideal at 16 threads and 4.4% at 64, even though the profiled gridding stage reaches 39.7x at 64 threads.
- **I/O diagnostic:** I/O is only about 1% of wall time in the largest case, but 99.66% of file requests are misaligned. The trace contains 280,024 reads below 1 MB, including 262,144 reads to the same file, supporting the diagnosis of serial metadata and small-request overhead rather than peak-bandwidth saturation.
- **Location-dependent carbon and cost:** The 32,768 x 32,768 analysis holds throughput constant and models South Africa at 0.672 kgCO2/kWh and USD 0.19/kWh versus Western Australia at 0.321 kgCO2/kWh and USD 0.27/kWh. Operational electricity contributes 90-95% of the carbon result, so Western Australia has higher Mvis/kgCO2. Capital expenditure contributes 89-92% of modelled cost, while Western Australia's higher tariff still yields lower Mvis/USD. Larger batches amortise embodied carbon and capital cost.
- **Modelled co-design scenarios:** The paper's statements that software efficiency could reduce energy by up to 81%, carbon by 97%, and total cost by 32% under Western Australia assumptions are scenario/model results, not measured speedups from an implemented optimisation. Current dynamic energy is about 15% of total; the proposed improved-utilisation scenario raises that share toward 80%. In that scenario, operational energy accounts for about 78% of total carbon and 25% of cost.
- **CPU/FPGA design-space result:** PREESM plots non-dominated CPU-only and CPU-FPGA mappings for latency versus idle-subtracted energy-to-completion and latency versus occupancy. Occupancy is `avg(CPU, avg(CLB, DSP, BRAM))`, combining CPU-core use with FPGA configurable-logic-block, DSP, and block-RAM use. The paper demonstrates that Pareto selection is possible but does not tabulate exact per-design values or compare scientific fidelity for those mappings.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
astroCAMP is a measurement and co-design layer around the complete imaging workflow rather than a replacement for any one algorithmic stage. Its WSClean+IDG case directly exercises visibility ingestion, GPU gridding and interpolation, sub-FFTs, w-tiling, host-device orchestration, and image production. Its metric separation is especially useful for an ML pipeline because it prevents a fast learned gridder or deconvolver from being judged only by kernel latency: end-to-end wall time, visibility or image throughput, energy, peak memory, data movement, carbon, and cost can be reported alongside reconstruction quality against a fixed reference.

For ML evaluation, the proposed quality tuple provides a starting point for combining dirty-image RMS, PSNR/SSIM, dynamic range, source-position error, and catalogue-level photometric measures with an explicit maximum acceptable loss. The standardised SKA-Low workloads and reference images could support comparisons across classical CPU/GPU implementations and learned components, while the paper's measured orchestration and static-energy overheads show why accelerator utilisation must be evaluated over the full pipeline timeline. However, astroCAMP reports no ML baseline and no measured fidelity trade-off, and its current quality suite lacks agreed survey-level tolerances. A learned visibility-to-image comparison would therefore need to supply those missing fidelity results and programme-specific thresholds rather than treating the paper's performance, energy, carbon, or cost curves as evidence that any approximation is scientifically acceptable.
