## One-line summary
astroCAMP defines a cross-layer benchmark and multi-objective co-design framework for comparing SKA-scale radio-imaging configurations by end-to-end performance, hardware behaviour, energy, carbon, cost, and scientific fidelity, and demonstrates a subset of those metrics on WSClean+IDG CPU/GPU runs and a model-based CPU/FPGA design-space exploration.

## Method
The framework separates benchmarking into three stages. First, it executes a fixed imaging workload under controlled conditions and records workflow timing, rack or node power, device telemetry, I/O, and resource counters. Second, it normalises those measurements into a common metric vector. Third, it explores algorithmic parameters, hardware choices, and workflow mappings as a multi-objective design space and identifies non-dominated configurations. A configuration is represented as `x = (a, h, s)`, where `a` covers choices such as precision, convolution-kernel size, w-stacking or NUFFT order, tiling, and task fusion; `h` covers processor and accelerator choices, memory hierarchy, power caps, and thread or block geometry; and `s` covers decomposition, data locality, I/O staging, buffering, batching, and orchestration.

The core optimisation minimizes time-to-solution, energy-to-solution, carbon-to-solution, and scalarised quality loss. Quality loss is `DeltaQ(x) = d(Q_ref, Q(x))`, a weighted distance from a high-fidelity reference in a quality space such as dirty-image RMS and PSNR. Feasible designs must remain within an allowed quality loss, an average-power cap, a minimum processing-throughput or survey-cadence requirement, and hardware resource bounds. Total cost of ownership and cost-to-solution are also part of the framework's metric backbone and objective space. The current implementation uses PREESM for system and platform design-space evaluation and CEO-DC for sustainability and economic calculations.

The released dataset uses OSKAR version `2.11.2-dev 2025-07-17 ddb65ed` and the full 512-station SKA-Low configuration. The paper enumerates 1, 8, 64, 128, and 256 timesteps and the same five channel counts, with 10 s per timestep, phase centre RA 25.0 degrees and Dec -30.0 degrees, a 151 MHz start frequency with 1 MHz channel increments, and GLEAM sources over a 40-degree field of view. It describes data volumes from megabytes to terabytes and supplies raw power and timing logs, energy metrics, a reference dirty image, and CPU/GPU configuration templates. The paper states that 16 datasets were produced, but also describes the five-by-five parameter grid and later says that 25 configurations at four image sizes produced 100 runs; these dataset counts are internally inconsistent.

The measured WSClean+IDG benchmark fixes the field of view and varies image size over 4096 x 4096, 8192 x 8192, 16,384 x 16,384, and 32,768 x 32,768 pixels, corresponding to 17.578, 8.789, 4.394, and 2.197 arcsec pixels. Each monitored run follows a CUDA-compiling warmup, a 120 s settling pause, the monitored execution, and another 120 s pause. Tests use exclusive access to a Lenovo ThinkSystem SR675 V3 KUMA node containing two AMD EPYC 9334 CPUs, 371 GB RAM, 6.4 TB NVMe, and four 94 GB NVIDIA H100 GPUs. Each run is allocated 16 CPU cores, one H100, and 92.2 GB RAM. Node PDU measurements use a 1 s power average sampled every 5 s and are cross-checked against CPU RAPL and GPU NVML through PMT. The economic model assumes a comparable server costs USD 29,875.39, embodies 513 kgCO2e including 176 kgCO2e for the H100, and amortises capital cost over six years.

The separate PREESM demonstration maps the inversion part of a DDFacet-like simulator across CPU-only and CPU-FPGA designs on a KRIA KR260 system-on-module. It uses a four-core 1.6 GHz ARM Cortex-A53 and a Xilinx Ultrascale+ FPGA at 150 MHz, maps a 128 x 128 two-dimensional FFT as two one-dimensional FFTs, and produces about 98,000 visibilities. It measures latency, resource occupancy, and energy above the board's idle baseline, then plots Pareto fronts for latency versus energy and latency versus occupancy.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **Metric roles and measurement types:** Table 2 is the framework's core contribution: 12+ cross-layer co-design metrics. Core (C) metrics enter the performance–energy–quality–cost optimisation; diagnostic (D) metrics explain bottlenecks. Metrics are typed as directly measured, derived, or proxy/model-based. The suite spans system/pipeline KPIs, hardware behaviour, algorithmic fidelity, sustainability, and economics.
- **Important scope split:** Stage 2 of the released workflow evaluates system/platform and sustainability/economic metrics (PREESM, CEO-DC). The paper states that algorithm-level quality metrics are intentionally excluded from the current experimental release pending community agreement on survey-level tolerances — but they are still defined as core Table 2 metrics and appear in the co-design formulation (quality tuple `Q(x)`, quality-loss constraint `ΔQ`). Classification below flags both measured and framework-defined core metrics.
- **Time-to-solution:** `T_c`, seconds, core and direct — full-system end-to-end wall-clock time (POSIX timing, workflow logs, or scheduler timestamps). Measured extensively on WSClean+IDG.
- **Energy-to-solution:** `E_c = ∫_0^{T_c} P(t) dt`, joules, core and direct — integrates system power (PDU, RAPL, NVML/PMT). Measured on the heterogeneous node runs.
- **Throughput:** `Θ = N / T_c`, visibilities/s (or images/s), core and derived.
- **Energy efficiency:** `η_E = N / E_c`, visibilities/J, core and derived.
- **Data locality / device utilisation / memory bandwidth / memory efficiency / peak memory:** diagnostic hardware metrics in Table 2 (local I/O fraction, active-time utilisation, GB/s, GB/J, `M_peak`).
- **Dirty-image RMS:** `σ_dirty = sqrt((1/N) Σ (I_i − Ī)²)`, Jy/beam, core — noise/artefact level in the dirty image (CASA/WSClean imstat, PyBDSF). Framework core fidelity metric; used in the quality tuple example. Residual RMS `σ_res` also appears in the DR definition below; neither is tabulated in Section 6. No R2D2-style normalized RDR (`‖r̂‖₂/‖x_dirty‖₂`).
- **PSNR / SSIM:** Table 2 core pair. PSNR compares reconstruction `Î` to reference `I_ref` via mean-squared error (rendered formula `10 log10(I_max² / MSE)` in dB; HTML table text sometimes drops the square). SSIM is dimensionless structural similarity. Both are named as core algorithmic-quality metrics and appear in the co-design quality examples; not numerically reported for the WSClean+IDG experimental matrix in this release.
- **Dynamic range:** `DR = I_max / σ_res`, core — peak intensity over residual-image RMS for faint-emission detectability. Framework-defined; not tabulated as a measured result in Section 6.
- **Astrometric / photometric error:** catalogue position and flux offsets vs reference — named with dirty-image RMS, PSNR/SSIM, and DR as the algorithmic-quality set that captures scientific impact of approximations (precision, w-stacking, NUFFT truncation).
- **No standalone SNR or NMSE:** the paper does not define a separate linear SNR or NMSE/NRMSE column. Reconstruction error enters via MSE inside PSNR, plus dirty-image/residual RMS.
- **Carbon-to-solution / carbon efficiency / TCO / cost per job / cost efficiency:** sustainability and economic Table 2 metrics; carbon and cost are modelled for location-dependent SDP scenarios.
- **Measured WSClean+IDG findings (system/compute):** GPU/IDG-device layers ≈60–90% of active-compute energy at 32,768²; static energy ≈80–85% of total; CPU end-to-end utilisation ≈2–18% (1–64 threads); strong-scaling wall times (e.g. 3:13:10 → 1:08:06 on 16,384²); I/O ≈1% of wall time but highly misaligned small reads; location-dependent Mvis/kgCO₂ and Mvis/USD (South Africa vs Western Australia).
- **CPU/FPGA PREESM DSE:** Pareto fronts for latency vs energy and latency vs occupancy on KRIA KR260; no tabulated scientific-fidelity numbers for those mappings.
- **Open community call:** Section 7.2 asks the SKA community to define quantitative application-level quality metrics and tolerances (PSNR/SSIM, flux-scale accuracy, astrometry, polarisation purity, RM recovery, spectral-line fidelity, time-series accuracy, transient completeness, etc.).

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
astroCAMP is a measurement and co-design layer around the complete imaging workflow rather than a replacement for any one algorithmic stage. Its WSClean+IDG case directly exercises visibility ingestion, GPU gridding and interpolation, sub-FFTs, w-tiling, host-device orchestration, and image production. Its metric separation is especially useful for an ML pipeline because it prevents a fast learned gridder or deconvolver from being judged only by kernel latency: end-to-end wall time, visibility or image throughput, energy, peak memory, data movement, carbon, and cost can be reported alongside reconstruction quality against a fixed reference.

For ML evaluation, the proposed quality tuple (dirty-image RMS, PSNR/SSIM, dynamic range, catalogue errors) is meant to sit beside time/energy/carbon/cost objectives with an explicit maximum acceptable loss. The standardised SKA-Low workloads and reference dirty images could support classical vs learned comparisons once community tolerances exist. The current experimental release emphasises orchestration, utilisation, and sustainability bottlenecks rather than measured fidelity trade-offs, and it reports no ML baseline.
