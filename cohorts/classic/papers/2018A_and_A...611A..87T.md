**Bibcode:** 2018A&A...611A..87T

## One-line summary
DDFacet combines image-plane faceting, facet-dependent noncoplanarity corrections, direction-dependent PSFs, and wideband spectral deconvolution to reconstruct flux density and spectral index in the presence of generic direction-dependent Jones terms, while using multicore parallelism, baseline-dependent averaging, and early-cycle visibility sparsification to reduce gridding cost.

## Method
DDFacet partitions the field into regular square or irregular Voronoi facets and projects them onto one common tangent plane. Per-facet coordinate transformations remove the dominant noncoplanar phase gradient, while facet-dependent modified W-projection kernels correct the remaining within-facet w-term. The gridder and degridder apply externally supplied, spatially discrete time-frequency-baseline-dependent Jones matrices or primary-beam models. Image normalization and local, direction-dependent spectral PSFs account for the spatially varying response, including effects from time and bandwidth averaging.

The paper introduces two wideband deconvolvers. Hybrid Matching Pursuit (HMP) finds the brightest residual location and jointly fits all selected spatial and spectral basis coefficients there, optionally with non-negative least squares, before updating the model and residual. SubSpace Deconvolution (SSD) identifies bright pixel islands, jointly optimizes each island against its local spectral PSF, then subtracts the union of island models from the visibilities and re-images; the demonstrated SSDGA variant uses a genetic algorithm with an L2 fitness. Both operate on a cube of frequency-chunked dirty images and estimate intrinsic flux density and spectral index while incorporating local direction-dependent PSFs.

The implementation is mostly Python 2.7 with C gridding and degridding kernels. Facets are processed asynchronously with shared-memory multiprocessing. On-the-fly baseline-dependent averaging (BDA) applies per-visibility phase rotations and Jones terms, then grids once per allowed averaging domain. A separate sparsification option randomly omits visibility blocks in early, high-SNR major cycles and restores the full data for the final cycle.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **Relative flux-density error:** the simulation measures `(estimated S_50 - true S_50) / true S_50` at 50 MHz versus distance from the beam center, normalized by the 50 MHz half-power radius. Direction-independent narrowband imaging develops a strong negative radial bias. Direction-dependent narrowband imaging removes the main bias but retains substantial scatter. Direction-dependent HMP-WB and SSD-WB cluster much more closely around zero, with SSD visually tighter in Figure 9.
- **Spectral-index error:** the paper plots `estimated alpha_50 - true alpha_50` against normalized beam radius. Direction-independent wideband imaging is increasingly biased away from the beam center, while HMP-WB and SSD-WB recover spectral indices around zero error; the authors describe SSD as slightly better. The plots, rather than a table, provide these error results, so the paper reports no aggregate RMSE, median error, or confidence interval.
- **Residual artifacts and dynamic range:** restored-image comparisons show severe residual structure without beam or wideband correction, lower residuals after direction-dependent correction, and the cleanest image with SSDGA wideband deconvolution. The authors state that HMP or SSD increases dynamic range and reduces flux-density and spectral-index error, but they do not tabulate residual RMS or achieved dynamic range.
- **Fidelity-test context:** the single semi-realistic LOFAR LBA simulation uses 630 baselines from 36 stations over 2.8 h, five channels spanning 30-70 MHz, and an approximately 5.77 degree station-beam HPBW at 60 MHz. The sky has about 100 point sources with 50 MHz flux densities `S_50 = s^1.5` Jy for `s` uniformly distributed from 0 to 100, spectral indices uniformly distributed from -1 to 1, and an additional off-axis 10,000 Jy source. The applied station beam and synthetic direction-dependent Jones terms have typical amplitude variation 0.1 and phase variation 0.1 pi. Images are 8019 x 8019 pixels with 10 x 10 arcsec cells, and wideband residual cubes use three channels.
- **End-to-end multicore scaling:** a separate 14 h VLA C+D-configuration L-band observation of 3C147 contains 2,350,127 time-baseline samples, 64 channels over 256 MHz, and a 5100 x 5100 image covering 2.8 x 2.8 degrees with 529 facets and two 128 MHz bands. Five HMP major cycles clean to 0.4 mJy. On two Sandy Bridge Intel Xeon E5 CPUs with 8 physical and 16 virtual cores each, the single-core wall time is about 12 h, 94% of it in gridding. The run scales approximately linearly through 16 physical cores and reaches 12x speedup at 16 cores; the paper interprets this as 98% of computation parallelized. Scaling degrades once hyperthreads share physical cores.
- **BDA efficiency:** for a VLA B-configuration test, compression ratio is plotted against facet count at decorrelation limits 0.001, 0.01, and 0.04. The resulting per-facet gridding speedup rises to roughly 4-5x and then flattens at about 4x because memory access becomes limiting. These plots test computational savings, not reconstruction fidelity at each setting.
- **Sparsification trade-off:** for a representative VLA or MeerKAT L-band observation with 1e6-1e7 time-baseline samples per channel and dynamic range near 1e4 or higher, the paper argues that a sparsification factor of 100 reduces SNR by 10x while leaving early cycles above the noise floor. A typical five-cycle schedule is 100, 100, 30, 10, 1. This is an operational rationale, not a measured end-to-end speedup benchmark.
- **Scope limitation:** fidelity is demonstrated on one simulated point-source field, without a numerical comparison against another imager and without injected thermal-noise statistics. The paper does not benchmark HMP runtime against SSDGA runtime, does not report scalar image-error summaries, and leaves polarization-deconvolution errors from its Stokes-I PSF approximation for future work.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
DDFacet is a strong classical end-to-end reference for all four stages: it applies direction-dependent calibration terms during visibility gridding, produces per-facet FFT-ready grids, forms frequency-chunked dirty-image cubes and local PSFs, and performs wideband deconvolution for intensity and spectral index. Its evaluation suggests that an ML replacement should be tested not only on residual or image error, but also on radial flux bias, spectral-index error, position-dependent PSFs, decorrelation tolerance, and wall-clock scaling under realistic Jones terms. HMP and SSD also provide interpretable templates for learned minor cycles: a model could replace their local coefficient or island optimizer while retaining exact visibility-domain major-cycle corrections and DDFacet's faceted measurement operator.
