# The R2D2 Deep Neural Network Series Paradigm for Fast Precision Imaging in Radio Astronomy

**Bibcode:** 2024ApJS..273....3A
**Authors:** Amir Aghabiglou, Chung San Chu, Arwa Dabbech, Yves Wiaux
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJS..273....3A/abstract
**Full text:** https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4365/ad46f5/pdf
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05452
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4365/ad46f5

## One-line summary
R2D2 replaces CLEAN's handcrafted minor-cycle model selection with a short, sequentially trained series of residual-predicting DNNs that repeatedly receives the current image and back-projected visibility residual; on 512 x 512 simulated VLA problems, R2D2 and its nested data-model-informed R3D3 variant improve S/N and faint-structure log S/N over multiscale CLEAN, uSARA, and AIRI while reconstructing in seconds, although residual data fidelity remains weaker than the best baselines in high-dynamic-range tests.

## Method
The paper starts from noisy nonuniform Fourier visibilities and forms a normalized dirty image by applying the real part of the adjoint measurement operator. The corresponding image-domain normal operator includes Fourier degridding and gridding, while the point-spread function is normalized to peak at one. At R2D2 iteration `i`, the exact current residual dirty image is the dirty image minus the normal operator applied to the current image estimate. A DNN takes that residual and the current estimate as two image channels and predicts an additive residual image. Starting from a zero image, the reconstruction is the sum of these learned residuals. This gives R2D2 a hybrid structure: it resembles CLEAN's matching-pursuit update but learns the model component from both the image and dirty residual, while retaining an explicit data-consistency update between learned steps.

The DNNs are trained sequentially rather than as one jointly optimized unrolled network. The first network learns from ground-truth and dirty-image pairs. Each later network learns from the ground truth, the preceding reconstruction, and its residual dirty image, using an l1 reconstruction loss after projection onto the nonnegative orthant. RMSProp is used, and each network is initialized from the preceding network's learned parameters. Training stops when validation reconstruction metrics stabilize, so the inference series length is fixed during training rather than tuned for each test image. Iteration-specific normalization divides each training or inference triplet by the mean of the current image estimate, or by the dirty-image mean at the first iteration.

Two related incarnations are studied. The plain R2D2 uses a 31-million-parameter U-Net for each learned residual. Its encoder-decoder processes two 512 x 512 input channels, uses 3 x 3 convolutions, LeakyReLU, average pooling, transposed convolutions, and skip connections, and outputs one residual-image channel. R2D2-Net instead unrolls several U-Net residual updates inside one component, interleaving them with approximate data-fidelity layers implemented as image-domain PSF convolutions. A series whose outer components are these internally unrolled R2D2-Nets is called R3D3. The experiments train plain R2D2 for 15 outer iterations and R3D3 variants with three- and six-layer R2D2-Net components for 7 and 8 outer iterations, respectively.

Training is telescope-specific to VLA configurations A and C. The 20,000 training pairs use curated, denoised, transformed medical and optical images; the 250 validation pairs use radio images, providing a modality split intended to test generalization. All images are 512 x 512. Ground-truth dynamic range is randomized from `1e3` to `5e5`; simulated VLA pointings, observation times, one to four frequency channels, bandwidth ratios, sampling-pattern rotations, and up to 20% flagging are varied. Sampling patterns contain `2e5` to `2e6` Fourier points. The operators use Briggs weighting with robustness zero and a 1.5 super-resolution factor, and Gaussian visibility noise is adapted to target image dynamic range. Once a training example's residual reaches its image-domain noise level, it is pruned from later DNN training; at least 40% of the original set is removed by the final component.

The main generic test contains 200 inverse problems, with 50 transformed ground-truth images from each of 3C 353, Messier 106, A2034, and PSZ2 G165.68+44.01 and independently varied VLA sampling. Baselines are the sparsity-based uSARA optimizer, the plug-and-play AIRI algorithm, and multiscale CLEAN in WSClean. uSARA and AIRI use noise-based parameter heuristics with batch-level adjustments; CLEAN is run deeply with auto-mask `1.5` and auto-threshold `0.5` times the estimated noise. Four additional experiments each contain 100 inverse problems and isolate low versus high image dynamic range, wider bandwidth, and longer observations. The study is simulation-only; the associated real-VLA demonstration is explicitly deferred to the sister Cygnus A paper.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **Linear S/N against ground truth:** `20 log10(||x_true||2 / ||x_true - x_hat||2)` in dB. On the generic 200-problem test, multiscale CLEAN obtains `13.6 +/- 3.6 dB`, uSARA `30.8 +/- 1.9 dB`, AIRI `31.3 +/- 2.3 dB`, R2D2 `33.7 +/- 1.5 dB`, R3D3-3L `33.8 +/- 1.4 dB`, and R3D3-6L `34.0 +/- 1.6 dB`. Across the three learned-series methods, the mean gain is `2.4-2.7 dB` over AIRI and `2.9-3.2 dB` over uSARA in this matched simulated test. A single end-to-end U-Net reaches only `20.5 +/- 2.7 dB`; the one-component data-informed R2D2-Net-3L and R2D2-Net-6L reach `32.6 +/- 1.5 dB` and `33.7 +/- 1.7 dB`, showing that both embedded data consistency and the outer residual series matter.
- **Log S/N against faint ground-truth structure:** S/N is recomputed after a logarithmic image transform parameterized by the known ground-truth dynamic range. Generic-test means are `10.3 +/- 3.5 dB` for CLEAN, `21.9 +/- 3.3 dB` for uSARA, `21.9 +/- 4.4 dB` for AIRI, `25.0-25.1 +/- 4.9 dB` for R2D2 depending on NUFFT implementation, `25.3 +/- 4.6 dB` for R3D3-3L, and `25.3 +/- 4.7 dB` for R3D3-6L. The end-to-end U-Net is only `6.6 +/- 3.3 dB`; R2D2-Net-3L is `19.6 +/- 5.4-5.5 dB`, and R2D2-Net-6L is `24.0 +/- 4.7 dB`.
- **Residual-to-dirty-image ratio:** Image-domain data fidelity is `||r_hat||2 / ||x_dirty||2`, with lower values indicating a cleaner back-projected residual. Table 2 reports means in units of `1e-4`: CLEAN `5.1 +/- 5.2`, uSARA `6.5 +/- 8.2`, AIRI `6.4 +/- 8.0`, R2D2 `13.5 +/- 46.9`, R3D3-3L `7.6 +/- 7.6`, and R3D3-6L `7.9 +/- 7.8`. Thus CLEAN has the lowest mean ratio in the generic experiment, uSARA, AIRI, and both R3D3 variants are comparable, and plain R2D2 is nearly twice the R3D3 level with much larger dispersion. In the targeted experiments, all methods are similar at dynamic range `5e3`; at dynamic range `1e5`, AIRI and uSARA have the lowest ratios, CLEAN is 2-4 times higher, R3D3 is 3-5 times higher, and R2D2 is another 20%-60% above R3D3. The paper therefore distinguishes reconstruction fidelity to known truth from data-residual fidelity rather than treating them as interchangeable.
- **Qualitative image and residual assessment:** R2D2, R3D3, AIRI, and uSARA recover broadly comparable compact and extended morphology, while CLEAN is smoother because its model is restored with a CLEAN beam. The end-to-end U-Net produces over-smoothed images and visible hallucinated structure in some cases. Those artifacts disappear in the iterative R2D2 series, and the data-informed single R2D2-Net does not show them. Conversely, R2D2 and R3D3 retain discernible residual structure around the brightest emission, especially in high-dynamic-range cases, consistent with their worse residual ratios. These are visual findings, not separately quantified morphology or hallucination metrics.
- **Iterations and reconstruction time:** Generic-test averages use one CPU core for CLEAN and uSARA; AIRI and the MATLAB R2D2-family runs use one CPU core plus one GPU; Python R2D2-family runs are measured both with that hybrid allocation and fully on one GPU. CLEAN takes `9 +/- 1` major cycles and `65.9 +/- 18.8 s`; uSARA takes `1103 +/- 373` iterations and `4184.2 +/- 1548.9 s`; AIRI takes 5000 iterations and `3478.8 +/- 1531.4 s`. R2D2 uses 15 iterations and takes `12.2 +/- 2.8 s` in MATLAB, `18.6 +/- 5.9 s` in hybrid Python, or `2.9 +/- 0.3 s` fully on a GPU. R3D3-3L uses 7 iterations and takes `9.4 +/- 1.5 s`, `9.8 +/- 4.3 s`, or `1.9 +/- 0.5 s`; R3D3-6L uses 8 iterations and takes `15.2 +/- 2.1 s`, `11.3 +/- 3.9 s`, or `2.2 +/- 0.3 s` under the same three implementations. The authors describe the contrast as seconds versus about an hour for uSARA/AIRI, but explicitly call exact timing comparisons indicative because languages, NUFFT implementations, and hardware allocation differ.
- **Per-step timing and implementation context:** Table 2 also separates average data-fidelity and regularization time. For example, MATLAB R2D2 averages `0.4 +/- 0.2 s` for data fidelity and `0.2 +/- 0.07 s` for its DNN step per iteration, while fully GPU Python R2D2 averages `0.05 +/- 0.01 s` and `0.1 +/- 0.2 s`. The paper notes that uSARA and AIRI could be further parallelized, WSClean is optimized for much larger problems, and GPU memory may force image or data decomposition at future telescope scale. No empirical SKA-scale memory or throughput benchmark is supplied.
- **Training compute and hardware:** Training runs on the Cirrus UK Tier-2 system. CPU nodes have dual 18-core Intel Xeon E5-2695 processors and 256 GB memory; GPU nodes have dual 20-core Intel Xeon Gold 6148 processors, four NVIDIA Tesla V100-SXM2 16 GB GPUs, and 384 GB memory. Plain R2D2 uses 15 components, 398 cumulative epochs, 4 GPUs and 6 CPU cores, costing 160 GPU-hours plus 4757 CPU-hours. R3D3-3L uses 7 components, 605 cumulative epochs, 12 GPUs and 6 CPU cores, costing 1291 GPU-hours plus 2165 CPU-hours; R3D3-6L uses 8 components, 591 cumulative epochs, 12 GPUs and 6 CPU cores, costing 1276 GPU-hours plus 2244 CPU-hours. R2D2 thus uses about 80% fewer training GPU-hours than either R3D3 realization but about twice their CPU-hours because it has almost twice as many outer components.
- **Scope and limitations:** The reported fidelity numbers rely on simulated VLA data with known 512 x 512 truth, Briggs robustness zero, a fixed 1.5 super-resolution factor, Gaussian noise, and training/test observation settings drawn from the same telescope-specific parameter family. There is no SSIM, PSNR, source completeness, astrometric error, flux-density bias, effective-resolution estimate, visibility-domain chi-square, calibrated real-data metric, or uncertainty metric. The paper itself identifies residual structure, fixed pixel resolution and weighting during training, GPU-memory limits, and the need for all-telescope generalization and faceting as future work.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
This is a direct blueprint for an ML deconvolution stage after visibility weighting, gridding, and inverse Fourier back projection. Its learned modules never operate on an image alone: each receives the current reconstruction and the latest dirty residual, and the outer loop recomputes that residual through the interferometric normal operator. Plain R2D2 therefore offers a learned CLEAN-like minor cycle with exact model-based residual updates, while R3D3 shows a speed-oriented alternative that embeds PSF-convolution approximations inside each learned component and restores exact consistency in the outer series.

The evaluation also gives this pipeline a concrete matched benchmark design. A comparison should retain visibility sampling and count, weighting, noise, pixel size and super-resolution, target dynamic range, image morphology, iteration definition, NUFFT backend, language, and CPU/GPU allocation; it should report ground-truth S/N, faint-structure log S/N, residual-to-dirty ratio, visual residual structure, and total plus per-step time together. The paper's main trade-off is especially relevant: R2D2-family models improve truth-based image fidelity and speed, but high S/N alone does not guarantee a noise-like or low-energy data residual, so any visibility-to-image ML system should preserve an independent data-consistency metric and test behavior beyond the telescope and weighting distribution used for training.
