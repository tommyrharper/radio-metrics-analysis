**Bibcode:** 2023ApJ...943..144M
**Authors:** Lia Medeiros, Dimitrios Psaltis, Tod R. Lauer, Feryal Özel
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...943..144M/abstract
**Full text:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.01667
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.01667

## One-line summary
PRIMO reconstructs sparse EHT observations by fitting a compact, simulation-trained principal-component image basis directly to visibility amplitudes and closure phases; on three synthetic 2017 M87 EHT data sets it recovers ring and turbulent structures at finer apparent resolution than a simplified 20 microarcsec comparison image, including for one simulation outside the training ensemble, but the paper does not benchmark against an actual CLEAN or regularized-maximum-likelihood reconstruction.

## Method
PRIMO learns an orthogonal image dictionary from 30,720 general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) and radiative-transfer snapshots. The training library comprises 30 simulation runs: 1024 snapshots each from magnetically arrested disk (MAD) and standard and normal evolution (SANE) models, five electron-density scales from 10^5 to 10^6 cm^-3, and ion-to-electron temperature parameters Rhigh = 1, 20, and 80. The library fixes inclination at 17 degrees, black-hole spin at 0.9, and initial black-hole mass at 6.5 x 10^9 solar masses. Each 64 GM/c^2 field has 1/8 GM/c^2 pixels, approximately 0.5 microarcsec for M87, and snapshots are separated by 10 GM/c^3, approximately 3 days 17 hours. GRay performs GPU-based ray tracing on high-resolution 3D HARM simulations.

Before principal-component analysis (PCA), each snapshot is low-pass filtered with a second-order Butterworth filter of radius 15 Glambda to remove scales beyond those probed by the EHT, then normalized to a common total flux. The images are neither mean-subtracted nor standardized by their variance. Ten components explain 98% of the training-library image variance, and 20 explain 99%. Because the image-domain PCA basis Fourier-transforms into the corresponding visibility-domain basis, PRIMO fits a linear combination of the eigenimages directly at the sampled interferometric coordinates rather than first gridding visibilities into a dirty image.

The visibility-domain PCA model is implemented in the MARkov Chains for Horizons (MARCH) Markov chain Monte Carlo code. With N eigenimages it has N + 3 free parameters: N - 1 relative component amplitudes, total compact-source flux, angular scale theta_g = GM/(Dc^2), sky position angle, and a binary image flip. A tapered two-dimensional sinc interpolator evaluates each numerical eigenvisibility at the observed (u,v) coordinates. The likelihood combines visibility-amplitude and closure-phase terms and assumes the individual measurements are independent.

For the proof-of-concept experiments, the compact zero-baseline flux is fixed at 0.6 Jy. The angular-scale prior is log-uniform over 1-6 microarcsec. The position-angle prior has modes aligned and anti-aligned with the M87 large-scale jet at 288 degrees East of North, each with width pi/8, and either flip has equal prior probability. Relative eigenimage amplitudes use Gaussian priors fitted to the training-library coefficients and broadened to twice their measured standard deviations. Baseline-dependent truncation errors measured from the training set are added as a theoretical model uncertainty, rescaled with theta_g and total flux.

Synthetic observations use the actual (u,v) locations and reported per-point thermal uncertainties of the 2017 April 5 M87 EHT data. Complex visibilities from a selected GRMHD snapshot are sinc-interpolated at those locations, then dithered with Gaussian noise. Gain errors are not simulated and station gains are not fit. The first two tests use snapshots from a MAD simulation with electron density 10^5 cm^-3, Rhigh = 20, spin 0.9, and mass 6.5 x 10^9 solar masses. The third test uses a SANE, Rhigh = 20, spin-0.7 simulation that was not in the spin-0.9 training ensemble. The first reported reconstruction uses 20 components and 10,000,000 MCMC steps.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **PCA compactness:** The cumulative fraction of training-library image variance explained is the basis-size metric: 10 components recover 98%, and 20 recover 99%. This measures dictionary compression over the 30,720 filtered simulation snapshots, not reconstruction accuracy from sparse data.
- **Visibility-domain truncation errors:** For reconstructions formed by projecting every training snapshot onto 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, or 35 components, the paper averages errors over azimuth and all 30,720 snapshots as a function of baseline length. Fractional complex error is the magnitude of the complex visibility difference divided by total flux. Fractional visibility-amplitude error is the absolute amplitude difference divided by total flux. Visibility-phase error is the wrapped absolute angular difference in degrees. All reconstructions are compared with the original unfiltered snapshots, and a 15 Glambda, second-order Butterworth-filtered curve shows the preprocessing error separately.
- **Reported truncation-error levels:** With 20 components, baselines below the approximately 8 Glambda maximum of the 2017 EHT array have fractional complex error below about 3%, fractional visibility-amplitude error below about 2%, and visibility-phase error below about 15 degrees. Near 1-4 Glambda, visibility nulls produce broad error peaks. Between visibility minima, the 20-component fractional complex error is about 2%. These are ensemble projection errors with complete simulated images, not errors obtained by fitting sparse noisy observations.
- **Synthetic visibility fit:** Visibility-amplitude and closure-phase plots compare each highest-likelihood reconstruction with the synthetic data. Residuals are normalized using observational and theoretical uncertainties added in quadrature. The authors report agreement without visible residual structure for the three examples, but provide no aggregate reduced chi-squared, held-out visibility error, or distribution over repeated noise realizations.
- **Image morphology and apparent resolution:** Image panels and horizontal and vertical cross-sections assess the location, width, and relative height of brightness peaks, ring size and width, central-depression size and depth, brightness-asymmetry orientation, and extended flux-tube structure. For context, the input is also blurred with a 15 microarcsec Gaussian chosen so that the median full width at half maximum over 128 radial cross-sections is 20 microarcsec, similar to published M87 image resolution. PRIMO is qualitatively closer to the original and Butterworth-filtered inputs than this blurred image and shows no substantial ring-size bias. This is not an actual CLEAN or regularized-maximum-likelihood baseline: the authors explicitly blur the ground-truth GRMHD image rather than reconstructing the simulated visibilities with either algorithm.
- **Out-of-training-ensemble test:** The third input changes the training library's fixed spin from 0.9 to 0.7 and uses a SANE model. PRIMO qualitatively recovers its central depression, emission-peak geometry, asymmetry orientation, and extended structure. This single parameter-shift example supports some basis generality but is not a broad out-of-distribution benchmark.
- **Posterior diagnostics:** Corner plots compare ground-truth parameters with highest-likelihood values and posterior intervals. Low-order component posteriors are narrower than their priors, indicating that the sampled data constrain broad image structure, while higher-order components are increasingly prior-dominated. The paper reports close ground-truth agreement qualitatively and discusses parameter covariances, but does not summarize calibration coverage, parameter bias, or error across a test set.
- **Missing quantitative image benchmark:** No image-domain mean squared error, normalized root mean squared error, PSNR, SSIM, normalized cross-correlation, dynamic range, source completeness, or effective-resolution transfer function is reported. The three demonstrations use synthetic black-hole images, 2017 M87 sampling, thermal noise only, fixed total flux, and no gain corruption. Consequently, claims about resolution and fidelity are supported by visibility projection errors, residual plots, cross-sections, and visual morphology rather than a controlled comparison against production imaging algorithms.
- **Compute and hardware context:** The paper states that all ray-tracing calculations used the University of Arizona El Gato GPU cluster and that GRay is massively parallel and GPU-based. It reports neither GPU model, MCMC hardware, wall-clock time, memory use, nor a PRIMO runtime or scaling benchmark, so computational efficiency is not quantitatively evaluated.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
PRIMO is a learned-dictionary alternative to the usual gridding, inverse Fourier transform, and image-domain deconvolution sequence. It trains PCA eigenimages in image space but Fourier-transforms them once and evaluates their linear combination directly at the measured sparse (u,v) coordinates. The inference loop therefore compares model visibility amplitudes and closure phases with the observations without making a dirty image or applying a conventional CLEAN minor cycle. Its data-driven component is linear PCA rather than a neural network, while its training distribution supplies a strong physics prior over plausible black-hole morphology.

For an ML visibility-to-image pipeline, the paper provides useful evaluation targets: complex-visibility, amplitude, and wrapped-phase error as functions of baseline length; residuals separated from model-truncation uncertainty; posterior sensitivity to training priors; recovery of ring diameter, width, central depression, asymmetry, and extended structure; and explicit tests outside the training parameter grid. It also highlights controls needed for a stronger benchmark: station gain errors, multiple noise realizations, held-out visibility coordinates, diverse source classes and arrays, matched CLEAN and regularized-maximum-likelihood baselines, scalar image-fidelity metrics, calibrated uncertainty coverage, and reported reconstruction hardware and runtime.
