# Deep radio-interferometric imaging with POLISH: DSA-2000 and weak lensing

**Bibcode:** 2022MNRAS.514.2614C
**Authors:** Liam Connor, Katherine L. Bouman, Vikram Ravi, Gregg Hallinan
**ADS:** https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.514.2614C/abstract
**Full text:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.03249
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03249
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stac1329

## One-line summary
POLISH adapts a WDSR residual super-resolution network to map a dirty radio image directly to a deconvolved image with twofold or threefold finer sampling; on 50 simulated DSA-2000 validation fields it beats standard image-plane CLEAN by 5.9-7.7 dB in mean PSNR and by 0.009-0.012 in mean SSIM, while its real VLA demonstration remains qualitative and the method provides neither visibility-domain data consistency nor reconstruction uncertainty.

## Method
POLISH is a supervised, feed-forward image-plane method. Training pairs follow a simplified measurement model in which a high-resolution sky is convolved with a point-spread function, corrupted by zero-mean independent Gaussian image noise, and spatially downsampled. The model modifies Wide Activation for Efficient and Accurate Image Super-Resolution (WDSR): 32 WDSR-b residual blocks and a global skip path process the dirty image, batch normalization is removed, and sub-pixel convolution with pixel shuffle upsamples the output by a factor of two or three. Inputs and targets can contain an arbitrary number of frequency channels and use 16-bit integers or 32-bit floats, although the reported DSA-2000 results use 16-bit, single-channel images. The network minimizes pixel-wise l1 loss; model checkpoints are selected by validation PSNR.

Rather than train against one exact PSF, the authors generate a distribution by applying three random, physically motivated transforms to an ideal CASA/WSClean PSF: radial stretching, spatial warping, and an affine distortion and translation. This is intended to make the learned inverse robust to calibration and pointing errors. The PSF is encoded only through the training examples and is not supplied explicitly at inference. The principal DSA-2000 training skies are about 2000 x 2000 pixels over about 0.3 x 0.3 deg, populated with elliptical Gaussian radio galaxies whose count, flux, size, ellipticity, orientation, and spectral-index distributions are intended to resemble observed populations. The dirty-image simulations use 0.5 microJy/pixel noise and threefold downsampling. Two observing responses are evaluated: a 1300 MHz full-band PSF with 15 minutes of Earth-rotation synthesis and a 10 MHz single-snapshot PSF using less than 1 percent of the planned band.

The main experiments use 800 training and 100 validation pairs. Training runs for roughly 1 million epochs with a decaying learning rate from 0.001 to 0.0005. Each model takes about 20 GPU h on an NVIDIA TITAN RTX with 24 GB memory; the project total is reported as about 300 GPU h. The primary baseline is standard image-plane CLEAN, not multi-scale CLEAN or a visibility-domain regularized maximum-likelihood method. For transfer to real data, a separate POLISH model is trained entirely on simulations made with the modelled PSF of a real 2-4 GHz VLA observation, 3 microJy/pixel simulated noise, and twofold upsampling, then applied to its 0.4 x 0.4 deg dirty image.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **Definitions and evaluation scope:** The paper uses PSNR in decibels as its pixel-wise truth metric and SSIM as its local structural or perceptual metric. PSNR is sensitive to astrometric shifts; SSIM compares local means, variances, and covariance. Inputs and targets are normalized to the available integer range before evaluation. CLEAN's computational cost limits the controlled comparison to 50 validation images, even though the validation pool contains 100 pairs.
- **Full-band DSA-2000 simulation:** With the 1300 MHz, 15-minute PSF, POLISH obtains mean PSNR 55.9 +/- 4.7 dB and SSIM 0.998 +/- 0.0016. Image-plane CLEAN obtains 50.0 +/- 6.0 dB and 0.989 +/- 0.007 on the same simulated radio-galaxy task. The reported mean improvements are therefore 5.9 dB PSNR and 0.009 SSIM.
- **Narrow-band DSA-2000 simulation:** With the more strongly structured 10 MHz snapshot PSF, POLISH obtains PSNR 55.1 +/- 3.8 dB and SSIM 0.988 +/- 0.0016, compared with 47.4 +/- 3.6 dB and 0.976 +/- 0.009 for CLEAN. The corresponding mean improvements are 7.7 dB and 0.012. These truth-based results use synthetic Gaussian-galaxy skies, 0.5 microJy/pixel added noise, and threefold output upsampling.
- **False detections:** Source-Extractor finds approximately one false-positive galaxy per 20 simulated galaxies in CLEAN and one per 50 in POLISH, equivalent to approximate false-positive incidences of 5 percent and 2 percent. The paper does not provide the underlying source counts, completeness, threshold settings, uncertainty intervals, or a matched precision-recall curve, so this is a supporting estimate rather than a fully specified catalog benchmark.
- **Super-resolution:** Source-Extractor fits semi-major and semi-minor axes for hundreds of simulated galaxies in the 1300 MHz reconstructions. POLISH sizes visually track truth into the sub-arcsecond regime below the roughly 3 arcsec DSA-2000 PSF width, whereas CLEAN sizes asymptote near 3 arcsec. The paper does not report an aggregate size RMSE, shear bias, uncertainty calibration, or a tested resolution limit; it explicitly notes that the smallest simulated galaxies approach the pixel scale.
- **PSF-mismatch ablation:** A network trained with warp parameter gamma from 0 to 20 is tested as far as gamma = 30 and a 1.4x radial PSF stretch. PSNR decreases outside the training distribution but remains above 50 dB across the displayed 3 x 3 grid. This is evidence of robustness to the selected synthetic perturbations, not a calibration-error benchmark on measured visibilities.
- **Real VLA transfer:** On a real 2-4 GHz observation from the 27-antenna VLA, POLISH visually removes radial PSF struts and speckle around bright galaxies more completely than CLEAN after training only on simulated VLA-PSF pairs. No known truth, PSNR, SSIM, flux-density error, source completeness, visibility residual, or blinded expert score is available for this experiment, and the paper describes the comparison qualitatively.
- **Compute and latency context:** The paper states that a 2000 x 2000 dirty image can be reconstructed in a few seconds on a laptop, but gives neither an exact timing protocol nor laptop hardware. Training takes about 20 GPU h per model on a 24 GB TITAN RTX and about 300 GPU h in total. CLEAN runtime is not reported, so the study does not provide a controlled end-to-end latency or energy comparison.
- **Weak-lensing forecast is not an imaging measurement:** The cosmology forecast assumes effective resolution of roughly 3 arcsec/SNR, that half of detected galaxies become shape-measurable with POLISH, and effective source densities of 3 per square arcminute with super-resolution versus 0.5 without it. The forecasted parameter contours do not validate shape recovery: multiplicative and additive shear bias, PSF leakage, source blending, and reconstruction uncertainty are deferred to future work.
- **Comparison limitations:** Numerical image fidelity is demonstrated only on simulations. The paper does not report CLEAN thresholds or other baseline tuning details, excludes multi-scale CLEAN and RML baselines, and independently scales some image panels for visualization. POLISH supplies no posterior uncertainty and, because it operates after dirty-image formation, no explicit visibility-domain data-consistency check.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
POLISH is directly relevant to the learned deconvolution stage but is not an end-to-end visibility-to-image model. Conventional weighting, gridding, and inverse FFT first collapse the visibilities into a dirty image; POLISH then replaces iterative deconvolution and restoration with one feed-forward super-resolution pass. This division is attractive when raw visibilities cannot be retained and low latency dominates, and PSF perturbations in the synthetic training generator provide a simple route for encoding instrument uncertainty.

The same placement creates important evaluation and systems requirements. Because the network is not explicitly conditioned on the actual PSF and never revisits visibilities, performance depends on the training distribution covering real array configurations, calibration errors, sky morphologies, noise correlations, bandwidth effects, and dynamic range. A production comparison should hold gridding, weighting, dirty-image normalization, pixel scale, CLEAN variant and tuning, and output restoration fixed; measure flux and astrometric error, completeness and false detections, effective resolution, shear bias, visibility residuals, uncertainty calibration, and inference wall time on named hardware; and test failures under out-of-distribution PSFs and non-Gaussian sky structure. The real VLA example is encouraging evidence of simulation-to-data transfer, but without quantitative real-data consistency tests it does not establish scientific fidelity for a visibility-to-gridding-to-iFFT-to-deconvolution pipeline.
