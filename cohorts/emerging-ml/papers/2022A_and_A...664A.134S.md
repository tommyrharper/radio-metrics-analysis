## One-line summary
The radionets proof of concept uses an eight-residual-block CNN to fill missing amplitude and phase samples on already-gridded Fourier planes before an inverse FFT; on 10,000 simplified synthetic radio-galaxy tests it recovers jet angle and thresholded source area more consistently than one fixed WSClean configuration and executes the learned model in 0.0031-0.4070 s for 64-1024 pixel images, but flux estimates have substantial scatter and no real visibility data are evaluated.

## Method
The paper trains a fully convolutional residual network to complete a regular 2D Fourier grid rather than deconvolve a dirty image. Complex Fourier values are represented as separate amplitude and phase channels, and unsampled grid cells, called frequels, are set to zero. A grouped 9 x 9 pre-convolution maps the two inputs to 64 channels, split equally between amplitude and phase. Eight residual blocks each contain two 3 x 3 convolutions, batch normalization, a PReLU activation, and a local skip connection. A 3 x 3 post-convolution and batch normalization are enclosed by another skip connection, and a final grouped 9 x 9 convolution returns completed amplitude and phase maps. An inverse FFT of those maps produces the reconstructed image. The architecture has no resizing operation and does not extract CLEAN components or apply a restoring beam.

Training skies are 64 x 64 pixel Monte Carlo images of one- or two-sided radio galaxies assembled from Gaussian components. A central core is joined by 3-6 one-sided jet components spaced by 5 pixels; mirroring produces 4-13 total components for two-sided objects. Source orientation, peak amplitude, and component width vary, but the morphology remains highly idealized. The skies are Fourier transformed and sampled with masks derived from simulated ten-antenna VLBA coverage. Maximum and minimum baselines correspond to scales of about 2 and 8 pixels, respectively, without an absolute angular interpretation. Instrumental effects, primary beams, sidelobes, correlated noise, non-coplanar terms, and ungridded measurement effects are omitted. Typical training inputs retain 18%-27% of Fourier-grid cells.

Each noise condition uses 50,000 training, 10,000 validation, and 10,000 test amplitude-phase pairs, with an independently generated sampling mask per sky and random 90 degree rotations for augmentation. Separate models are trained on noiseless inputs and on skies perturbed in image space by independent Gaussian noise scaled to 5% of peak source flux. A further test adds independent Gaussian noise with standard deviation 0.05 to both real and imaginary Fourier components. Training uses an amplitude L1 loss plus a phase L1 loss after restricting predicted phase to the interval from -pi to pi, ADAM, 300 epochs, batch size 64, and learning rate 2 x 10^-4.

The main baseline is WSClean, run with one fixed configuration for all 10,000 sources: 64 pixels, 0.39 milliarcsec scale, major-cycle gain 0.3, gain 0.005, and 50,000 iterations. This is not an identical reconstruction path. WSClean receives ungridded visibilities generated with a simple direction-independent RIME Fourier kernel and returns a point-source model convolved with its clean beam, whereas radionets completes masked, already-gridded Fourier planes and its native output is unconvolved. The authors explicitly describe the comparison as not completely fair and note that WSClean was not optimized per source or for images this small.

## Performance / fidelity metrics used
- **Jet-angle error on 10,000 simulated sources:** Jet orientation is estimated by principal-component analysis. Mean prediction-minus-truth offsets are -0.016 +/- 1.429 degrees for the noiseless-input network and -0.014 +/- 1.333 degrees for the image-noise-trained network. WSClean gives 2.037 +/- 27.441 degrees under the fixed settings. With additional Fourier-domain white noise, the network gives 0.05 +/- 1.96 degrees. These are errors against synthetic Gaussian-component truth, not astrometric tests on real sources.
- **Thresholded source-area ratio:** Source boundaries are contours at 10% of each source's peak, with enclosed areas computed numerically. Prediction-to-truth ratios are 0.964 +/- 0.117 for noiseless inputs and 0.992 +/- 0.128 for image-noisy inputs, compared with 0.869 +/- 0.372 for WSClean. The network ratio under additional Fourier white noise is 1.00 +/- 0.13. This metric combines missed source extent and background structure and depends on the chosen relative threshold.
- **Core specific-intensity deviation:** The brightest Gaussian component is detected and its pixels are averaged. Mean relative deviations are -4.354% +/- 6.662% for the noiseless network, 0.613% +/- 12.402% for the image-noise-trained network, and -13.645% +/- 24.312% for WSClean. With added Fourier white noise the network gives 3.49% +/- 12.26%. Intensities are in arbitrary units, and the large dispersions show that near-zero mean bias does not imply accurate per-source flux recovery.
- **Sampling-density stress test:** Without retraining, dedicated 10,000-image tests at 20%, 50%, and 70% Fourier-grid filling give jet offsets of -0.07 +/- 3.14, 0.02 +/- 1.96, and 0.01 +/- 3.12 degrees; intensity deviations of -8.99% +/- 16.26%, 6.69% +/- 14.45%, and 11.94% +/- 63.60%; and area ratios of 0.85 +/- 0.19, 0.92 +/- 0.17, and 0.91 +/- 0.17. The non-monotonic angle and flux results, especially the 70% flux dispersion, qualify the paper's broader statement that increased sampling improves reconstruction.
- **Mixed point and extended sources:** A separately trained model is evaluated on 10,000 64 x 64 images containing one to six extra point-like Gaussian sources plus extended galaxies. Figure 21 reports mean specific-intensity deviations of -5.856% +/- 21.548% for point sources and -13.88% +/- 16.191% for extended sources. The experiment finds no clear correlation between source extent and intensity deviation. It broadens the Gaussian simulation but remains far from a realistic radio sky.
- **Runtime and scaling:** Averaged over 100 runs on one image, pure network evaluation takes 0.0031 +/- 0.0001, 0.0081 +/- 0.0002, 0.0269 +/- 0.0008, 0.0995 +/- 0.0043, and 0.4070 +/- 0.0052 s at 64, 128, 256, 512, and 1024 pixels. Corresponding end-to-end radionets times, including model and data loading and output saving, are 2.29 +/- 0.04, 2.31 +/- 0.08, 2.48 +/- 0.06, 2.65 +/- 0.13, and 3.20 +/- 0.06 s; WSClean times are 0.43 +/- 0.02, 0.63 +/- 0.04, 1.03 +/- 0.04, 2.42 +/- 0.09, and 9.00 +/- 0.344 s. Thus pure inference is faster at every size, while the complete framework becomes faster only above 512 pixels. This is not an end-to-end benchmark from raw telescope data, and the two methods produce differently restored outputs.
- **Training compute and hardware:** Training takes about 170 s per epoch and just over 14 h for 300 epochs. Appendix C specifies an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2080 with 8 GB, a 12-core 3.7 GHz Intel Core i7-8700K, and a 512 GB SSD. The paper does not separate CPU and GPU utilization or energy. Fine-tuning from 64 x 64 to 128 x 128 inputs takes about 30 min for 20 epochs on 5,000 training and 1,000 validation maps, but no aggregate fidelity metric is reported for that transfer test.
- **Qualitative and missing measures:** Example residual images show under-recovered source intensity, low-level parallel artifacts, and degraded small-scale phase structure under noise. More complex bent and overlapping jets are reconstructed less accurately until fine-tuned, after which faint structure improves at the cost of more background artifacts. The paper reports no PSNR, SSIM, RMSE, source completeness or false-positive rate, visibility-domain residual or chi-square, calibrated dynamic range, real-data flux or astrometric error, effective angular resolution, or uncertainty calibration. A pixelwise Gaussian uncertainty loss is proposed and illustrated only as preliminary future work, not established as a benchmarked capability.

## Relevance to visibility→gridding→iFFT→deconvolution ML pipeline
Radionets places learning between gridding and the inverse FFT. Conventional acquisition or simulation must first supply a regularly sampled Fourier plane; the CNN then imputes its missing amplitude and phase values, and a single inverse FFT replaces the usual dirty-image formation plus iterative deconvolution and restoration. Its constant-resolution fully convolutional design permits larger grids after limited fine-tuning, and its millisecond-scale learned step shows the potential throughput of Fourier-domain completion when many observations share a tightly controlled distribution.

The study also exposes major requirements for a production visibility-to-image pipeline. The network does not accept raw irregular visibilities, model baseline-dependent noise or calibration, enforce that measured Fourier samples remain unchanged, or apply an explicit measurement operator during inference. Its training distribution fixes simplified Gaussian morphologies, VLBA-like masks, image scale, and noise assumptions, so the reported gains do not establish generalization to real arrays or skies. A stronger evaluation should use common ungridded visibilities, weighting and gridding; match pixel scale, restoration, and baseline tuning; preserve measured-data consistency; and report flux and position error, completeness, false detections, effective resolution, visibility residuals, uncertainty calibration, total wall time, and memory on named hardware.
